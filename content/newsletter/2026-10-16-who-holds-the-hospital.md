# Who Holds the Hospital

*Part 2 of a series on DHA's FY27 campaign plan, one line of effort at a time. DHA's FY27 campaign plan asks its largest hospitals to take on harder cases so uniformed clinicians stay ready for war. Those same hospitals have to keep running when the clinicians deploy. This issue follows Line of Effort 2 through the budget, the job postings, and the hiring rules that decide who is left in the building, and ends with fixes that work whichever way the staffing debate breaks.*

Friends,

The 82nd Airborne Division keeps one battalion of its ready brigade at Fort Bragg on alert to deploy within 18 hours of notification. [1] Womack Army Medical Center sits on the same post, running the clinics that soldiers, families, and retirees use every day. Some of the clinicians in those clinics wear the uniform too, and when the force needs them in the field, they go.

The Army manages that through the Professional Filler System, known as PROFIS, which lets military clinicians practice in a hospital between deployments and keep their skills current until a deploying unit needs them. [2] When RAND evaluated the system in 2013, medical personnel told the researchers that PROFIS deployments meant more work and less access to care at the hospitals they left behind. [2] The hospital is a training ground and a clinic at the same time, and the two jobs pull against each other on the day the plans come.

On October 7, Womack posted a job for a practical nurse in its outpatient clinics. The grade is GS-5, and under promotion potential the posting says "None." [3] It is the nineteenth practical nurse posting from Womack since January 2025, for family medicine, pediatrics, medicine, surgery, and the outpatient clinics, and all nineteen stop at the same grade with the same word. [4]

Both of those facts sit inside the plan Vice Adm. Darin Via released on October 1 for fiscal 2027, which this series is walking one line of effort at a time. Tuesday's issue took the first line, warfighter medical readiness.

The second, medical warrior currency, aims to bring more complex care into the Core 15, the fifteen military hospitals DHA calls its "largest readiness-generating platforms," so uniformed clinicians treat the kinds of cases they will see in war. [5] Its numbers are specific. By 2028, 85 percent of identified, deployable military medical personnel in military hospitals should meet all of their wartime knowledge, skills, and abilities standards. The Core 15 should see 20 percent more acuity and a 20 percent richer surgical case mix, 15 percent growth in what the plan calls TRICARE For Life enrollment, and 50 percent more direct care appointments for specialty TRICARE For Life patients, network referrals, and veterans. [5] Brig. Gen. James Burk leads the effort. Acuity measures how sick and complex the patients are, so the target asks for harder cases on top of more volume.

The people who keep those hospitals open when the clinicians deploy get tasks, with dates and no numbers. The Core 15 owe staffing plans "based on current authorizations" by October 31. The networks owe a manpower alignment against existing authorizations by December 31, and DHA owes a business case for using reservists at its hospitals before it turns to contracts, by March 31, 2027. [5] None of those tasks carries a target. The plan sets its own test for the gap: "All enterprise decisions will prioritize staffing and workflows and not increase risk to mission execution or outcomes." [5] The budget and the hiring record are where that sentence gets checked.

## Why the surgeons need the cases

LOE 2 starts from a shortage of the right patients. By GAO's count the department runs one Level I trauma center of its own, and from fiscal 2020 through 2024 military medical personnel logged about 723,800 hours in civilian partnerships to find the trauma and complex surgery their own hospitals do not see. [6] At home, the inpatient work tilts toward families. Childbirth is the top reason military hospital enrollees are admitted, and direct care handled 23,701 deliveries in FY24, about 16 percent of its 150,262 admissions. [7] A labor and delivery ward keeps a hospital busy and does little for a trauma team's currency.

Via laid out the answer in March, when he told military medical leaders that a core group of 15 hospitals would lead the currency effort, using VA and civilian partnerships to bring more complex cases inside military walls. [8] The complex cases are in the network. TRICARE For Life beneficiaries, the Medicare-eligible retirees, had 325,906 private sector admissions in FY24, about 2.2 times every admission in direct care. [7] LOE 2 goes after that volume, along with veterans through VA partnerships and the network referrals military hospitals get first claim on. The plan measures success in military clinicians who meet their wartime skill standards, so a civilian nurse practitioner who treats a complex patient adds to the hospital's caseload and does nothing for that measure. [5] It also wants that experience counted wherever it happens. By January 30, 2027, DHA owes a system to capture and score clinical work done outside its hospitals, from civilian partnerships to VA care, and by December 31 it owes agreements that let its providers practice at local VA medical centers. [5] GAO found that 39 percent of personnel whose partnership work showed on readiness dashboards had no partnership hours in the timecard system. [6]

Every one of those cases needs a team around the surgeon. The budget shows who DHA expects that team to be.

## Three budgets, three directions

For most of fiscal 2025, DHA could not hire. GAO reported that DHA officials said they were "unable to hire any civilian employees to help address the military medical personnel shortfall for the rest of fiscal year 2025," against a projected shortfall of more than 8,000 clinical military personnel. [9] The Department's Inspector General found DHA "removed funding from ongoing civilian hiring actions" for unfilled positions, and by August 8, 2025, 972 DHA employees had taken deferred resignation and 49 early retirement. [10][11]

Fiscal 2026 funded the rebound. In-House Care, the budget line that runs the military hospitals and clinics, adds 2,756 uniformed personnel to end strength and 1,905 civilian full-time equivalents in its FY26 column, the civilian growth "to overcome hiring lags and other workforce challenges encountered in FY 2025." The same column cuts 1,260 contractor FTEs. [12]

Fiscal 2027 points toward uniforms. Average uniformed strength in In-House Care rises by 1,486, mostly the FY26 additions carrying through a full year, while end strength rises 215. Civilian FTEs go from 41,969 to 41,961, a "targeted reduction" the justification says lets DHA reallocate resources "to strengthen core military capabilities." [12] Contract services fall $294.3 million and contractor FTEs hold at 13,572. A $52.8 million increase in civilian pay goes to retention on a civilian base that does not grow, with the stated aim of reducing reliance on contracted labor for "certain administrative, clinical support, and operational functions" inside military hospitals. [12]

By my math, that leaves In-House Care in FY27 about 48 percent uniformed, 39 percent civilian, and 13 percent contractor. [12] Some in military medicine argue for a 60/40 civilian-to-military split so the hospitals can absorb deployments. Counting contractors, the hospitals are already about 52 percent non-uniformed, and reaching 60 percent at FY27's size would mean shifting about 8,950 positions out of uniform. The uniformed share is moving the other way, from 47.2 percent in FY25 to 48.3 percent in FY27. Count only government employees and the picture changes again: CRS put the Military Health System at 56 percent military and 44 percent civil service in FY2024, and in the overseas facilities the Inspector General sampled, Army facilities were 56 percent civilian while Navy and Air Force facilities were 6 percent. [13][10]

Whether that drift is good news depends on which day you plan for.

## Two days to plan for

The first is deployment day, and the record has a version of it from the last long war. In 2009, Madigan Army Medical Center had 462 soldiers ordered to deploy under PROFIS in a single year, and its cardiology clinic lost two active-duty cardiologists in one summer. [14] Four of its nurse anesthetists deployed at once, and the Army activated six reservist anesthetists on 90-day rotations to cover them. "People were pulling three job titles," one officer said at the time. [14] The campaign plan reaches for the same tool, with the reserve business case due before any new contracts. Under its third line of effort, it also asks for Core 15 surge staffing requirements above current manning by April 30, 2027 and "alternate staffing solutions" for Core 15 shortfalls in a crisis by September 30, 2027. [5] Deployment day is in the plan, filed under a different line of effort, with dates and no number. If one in five uniformed staff at a hospital deploys at once, a workforce that is 48 percent uniformed loses about a tenth of its people that week, and the civilians and contractors who remain carry the patients still coming through the door. That is the cost RAND's respondents described. [2] On that reading, every point the uniformed share rises makes the day harder to absorb, and FY2025 showed what a thin civilian bench looks like.

The second is the year before deployment. A uniformed surgeon stays ready by operating, and where civilian surgeons take more of the caseload, the uniformed surgeons see fewer of the hard cases. On that reading, every point the uniformed share falls sends the force out less ready, and LOE 2 exists to stop that. The budget math leans the same way. Uniformed pay comes from the services' military personnel accounts, outside In-House Care, so a uniformed clinician does not draw on DHA's operating budget the way a civilian does, and the cost moves to the services. [12] GAO reported that DHA expected military staffing to begin recovering around 2027. [9] RAND added a caution for this side too: receiving units found some PROFIS clinicians not well integrated with the units they deployed with, so hospital time builds clinical skill without guaranteeing a ready team. [2]

Both readings are right about the day they describe. Neither works without a civilian bench deep enough to hold the clinic, and one of the jobs on that bench changed grade in 2025.

## Six percent

I pulled every practical nurse announcement, the GS-0620 series, posted by DHA and its military hospitals on USAJOBS from 2019 through October 9 of this year. [4] Announcements before 2022 are few, likely because many hospitals still posted through the services. From 2022 through 2024, between 83 and 88 percent topped out at GS-6 or GS-7. In 2024 it was 444 of 502.

In 2025 it was 63 of 195. This year it is 22 of 376. Six percent.

The other 486 announcements from 2025 and 2026 stop at GS-5, and every one lists promotion potential as "None." [4] A GS-5 job with a path to GS-6 says so, and a 2026 DHA posting that offers one reads "6." [15] The 486 come from across the map, with Womack, Madigan, Hawaii, Landstuhl, Walter Reed, and Vicenza among them, which suggests a decision made above the hospital level. The work did not shrink with the grade. A 2026 GS-5 posting at Walter Reed requires experience administering IV fluids and medications and lists setting up a sterile field among its duties, and a 2024 GS-6 posting at Lyster covers the same work. [16][17]

Postings show what DHA offers a new LPN. They say nothing about hires or about anyone already on the payroll, and I could not find a public explanation for the shift. A classification review, a new standard for entry-level hiring, and budget pressure are the likely candidates, each with a different fix. Two data points help weigh them: an entry-level strategy would usually post a path to GS-6, and these list none, and the GS-6 share kept falling in 2026, after FY26 funded the civilian rebound. [4]

The grade sits on top of a heavy load. DHA's family medicine staffing model, finalized July 2, 2025, gives each provider two clinical support staff and half a medical support assistant, and the Inspector General found clinics running below it, including one that reported six pediatricians working with seven technicians. [10] My read is that a GS-5 ceiling makes that gap harder to close, because the clinic hires from the same pool of nurses as every civilian hospital in its market. The budget, meanwhile, wants civil servants doing more of the clinical support contractors do today. [12]

The grade is one door. The other is the one an LPN walks through on the way to becoming a registered nurse.

## The door to RN

Consider an LPN who has spent years in a military clinic, earns an associate degree, and passes the RN licensing exam. Under OPM's qualification standard for the nurse series, a new RN with a bachelor's degree qualifies for GS-5 with no experience. A nurse with an associate degree or diploma needs "one full year of professional nursing experience" to qualify at all, because GS-5 is the standard's entry grade, and the LPN years do not count toward it. [18] The nurse DHA already trained has to find that first year of RN experience somewhere else. That nurse is someone a military hospital has already vetted, credentialed, and taught its own workflows, which is the kind of person who holds a clinic together on deployment day.

Congress took up the gap in the FY2025 defense authorization act. Section 716 lets hiring managers waive OPM's experience requirement for nurse and practical nurse applicants who meet set criteria, and the criteria start at a bachelor's degree. A Senate amendment proposed covering associate degrees; conferees adopted the House's bachelor's-only text. [13] MOAA backed the provision as a way to keep skilled nurses in military hospitals, and DHA's postings now spell out the waiver for current and former department nurses and LPNs who earned a bachelor's or graduate degree after starting with the department. [19][20] The shortcut from DHA LPN to DHA RN runs through a four-year degree.

Even a nurse who qualifies waits. DHA reported to Congress 115 days from tentative offer to start for nurse hires between November 2022 and September 2023, and listed four initiatives to shorten it, including a rapid hiring pilot and offers issued by the hospitals themselves. [21] The campaign plan adds a model for running security checks inside the hiring action to cut background check time, due March 31, 2027. [5]

That is the staffing half of LOE 2. The other half is the patients it wants to bring in.

## The patients the plan is counting on

The plan's retiree goal is 15 percent growth in what it calls TRICARE For Life enrollment, and the term needs a definition. TRICARE For Life has no enrollment. [22] The likely measure is TRICARE Plus, which assigns Medicare-eligible beneficiaries to a primary care manager at a military hospital and which TRICARE describes as limited by capacity. [23] Medicare-eligible TRICARE Plus enrollment was 189,421 in FY22, 188,527 in FY23, and 191,134 in FY24, and Brooke Army Medical Center lists its program as closed to new enrollment. [7][24] The Core 15 baseline is not public. Applied across the enterprise, 15 percent would mean about 28,700 more enrollees on a base that grew by about 1,700 in two years, and each one needs a staffed primary care slot. TRICARE Plus patients are seen on a space-available basis, so their access shrinks whenever the hospital's capacity does, including the weeks after its clinicians deploy. [23] The veteran target draws on the same slots, with a policy enabling veterans to use military hospitals due July 30, 2027 and measurable volume targets for each Core 15 hospital due May 30. [5]

The budget's workload forecast is flat. In-House Care's weighted admissions rise from 93,256 in FY26 to 93,641 in FY27, an increase of 0.4 percent, and weighted outpatient workload falls by about 543,000 relative value units. [12] Retiree care is missing from those tables by design, because the Medicare-Eligible Retiree Health Care Fund pays for it, and the fund's receipts for military hospital care rise from $1,889.9 million to $2,201.2 million, a change that can reflect accrual rates and cost as easily as patients. [12]

The budget was cleared for release April 21, more than five months before the campaign plan, and the 20 percent applies to the Core 15, where volume could concentrate inside a flat total. [12] The FY28 request will show which way the workload follows.

This is not the first time the department has redrawn who staffs its hospitals.

## The pendulum

The department's FY2020 budget proposed cutting 17,005 military medical billets, later revised to 12,801 and phased through FY2027, partly by "converting certain military billets to civilian billets." [25][26] Congress halted the reductions in the FY2023 NDAA, a halt MOAA describes as five years. [27][28] Seven budgets ago the plan was fewer uniforms and more civilians. This budget adds uniforms. A hospital commander staffs the building for wherever the pendulum stops, and the fixes worth making are the ones that hold at either end.

## Fixes that work at either mix

My suggestion is to set the civilian share by specialty, according to how often each one deploys. Madigan's cardiology clinic, a 41-person staff that lost two active-duty cardiologists in one summer, shows how hard a small specialty gets hit. [14] If surgeons and emergency staff deploy more often than radiology technicians, as I expect, a Core 15 hospital could keep its uniformed strength where wartime currency lives and build deep civilian benches in the specialties that rarely leave. PROFIS already manages who deploys from each hospital, so the records to start from exist. [2] The tradeoff is real: the services still need uniformed clinicians in the specialties that deploy less, and those clinicians need somewhere to keep current too.

Whatever mix DHA chooses, a published number lets everyone check it. A staffing measure beside the currency target, such as the share of deploy-critical positions at each Core 15 hospital with an identified backfill, fits the operating room dashboard the plan already requires, which reports staffed operating rooms every quarter starting June 30, 2027. [5] The reserve business case due in March could price civilian backfill beside the reservists. The department has tied hospital personnel requirements to a number before, in a December 2023 memo built around a 7 percent recapture goal. [13]

The LPN job can get a ladder. Where the duties include IV medications and sterile technique, DHA can post at GS-5 with promotion potential to GS-6, as it already does on some postings, and publish its rationale if the ceiling came from a classification review. [15] The plan already requires standardized position descriptions by March 31, 2027, adding circuit-rider travel, preceptor roles, and emergency-essential designations, and the practical nurse description can come out of that work with a GS-6 path. [5] The posting data is public, and anyone can rerun the count next quarter.

Congress can extend the Section 716 waiver to associate degrees in the FY2028 NDAA, using the text a Senate amendment proposed in 2024. [13] OPM could consider crediting practical nurse experience toward the RN year. Either change widens the civilian bench without taking a uniform out of the Core 15.

DHA's own 2025 report to Congress named four initiatives to cut the 115-day wait from offer to start it measured in 2022 and 2023. [21] Reporting that time for each Core 15 hospital at midyear would show whether they work, and a faster civilian hire helps at any mix.

The retiree target needs a definition and a baseline. DHA could publish what TRICARE For Life enrollment measures and where each Core 15 hospital starts, tie TRICARE Plus reopenings to staffed capacity, and break out Core 15 workload, including retiree care, in the FY28 budget exhibits.

The contract cut can be sequenced. Contracted clinical support can convert to civil service or uniformed staff as replacements arrive, with overlap written into the final option periods. FY27 adds no civilian positions, so the FY28 request is where that conversion would show. [12]

Inside a hospital, count clinical support staff per provider against DHA's model of two, by clinic, and send the gaps up the chain. [10]

## What to watch

The plan's own calendar starts first. Core 15 staffing plans are due October 31, the manpower alignment and VA agreements by December 31, the reserve business case and new position descriptions by March 31, the operating room dashboard by May 30 with staffed operating rooms reported each quarter from June 30, and crisis staffing solutions by September 30, 2027. [5] Most of what settles the debate outside DHA will be public within a year. The FY28 budget request will show whether In-House Care adds civilian positions or keeps growing uniformed strength, and whether Core 15 workload gets its own line. [12] The FY28 defense authorization markups will show whether anyone carries the associate-degree waiver forward. [13] USAJOBS will show, posting by posting, whether practical nurse jobs keep stopping at GS-5 or start offering a path to GS-6, and the TRICARE program evaluation will show whether Medicare-eligible TRICARE Plus enrollment moves off its plateau. [4][7] None of those requires anyone to win the 60/40 argument first.

## Back at Bragg

When the ready battalion at Fort Bragg gets the call, the clock runs 18 hours. Whoever deploys from the hospital needs complex cases waiting at home to stay ready for the next rotation, and LOE 2 is built to put them there. The morning after the planes leave, Womack's outpatient clinic still opens, staffed by whoever was hired to stay.

LOE 2 has a number for the first group. The second is still waiting for one, and the latest job posting for it says "None."

Let's roll.
