A small business just forced NIH to redo its homework on a $3 billion
recompete. If you sell scientific or technical services to the government,
the reasoning in this decision is worth more to you than the outcome.

On August 14, GAO sustained LJR Solutions' protest of RFP 75N98026R00042
(B-424487), the NIH Scientific and Technical Support Services IDIQ we track.
Proposals closed April 27. The performance period was supposed to start
September 15. That date is gone.

What NIH got wrong was not the evaluation. It was the decision to run the
work full and open in the first place.

GAO found three problems with that call. First, NIH wrote a market research
memo in April 2026 that never explained why three small businesses it had
itself found capable in 2024 could no longer meet the requirement. The memo
asserted it and did not show the work. Second, NIH held small businesses to a
standard no company could clear, which was performing the entire scope
without subcontractors. No large business could have done that either. Third,
NIH never documented that it considered a partial set-aside, which the rules
require before an agency goes full and open with a small business reserve.

GAO recommended that NIH conduct new market research if appropriate, then
decide whether the solicitation should be set aside fully or partially.

Here is the part that should get your attention. This was the second time.
LJR raised the same objection in December 2024 against the earlier version of
this RFP. NIH took corrective action and GAO dismissed that protest as
academic. NIH came back in 2026, made the same call again, and put thin
documentation behind it.

**What this means for your capture timing.** GAO sustains are not common. In
fiscal 2025 it decided 380 protests on the merits and sustained 53, about 14
percent ([GAO's annual report to
Congress](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-900695)). When a sustain lands
on a set-aside determination instead of an evaluation, the delay usually runs
longer than a re-evaluation, because the agency may have to redo market
research and possibly re-solicit.

If you are a small business in bioinformatics, computational biology, BSL 2-4
lab research support, or clinical research support, the door you were locked
out of is open again. Get your capability statement in front of the NIAID
contracting shop while the market research is being redone. That is the
moment when being on the list changes the outcome.

If you bid as a large prime, your September start slipped and you should
reforecast the revenue. If you were a no-bid because the full and open
structure made the teaming math impossible, watch for the new market research
notice. This is one of the few honest second chances federal procurement
hands out.
