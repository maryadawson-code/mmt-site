The sustain I want you to read this month is not about an evaluation error.
It is about the question that comes before any evaluation: should this
requirement have been set aside at all. On August 14, 2026, GAO sustained
LJR Solutions, LLC's protest of the terms of NIH's request for proposals
75N98026R00042 (B-424487), the professional, scientific and technical
services recompete I track on the Contract Tracker as NIH's $3B
multiple-award IDIQ. LJR is a small business out of Myrtle Beach, South
Carolina. It argued the procurement should have been set aside for small
businesses. GAO agreed that NIH had not done the work to say otherwise.

**What GAO actually found.** The digest is short and worth quoting in
substance: NIH's assessment of whether it could reasonably expect fair-priced,
quality and timely offers from two or more responsible small businesses was
unreasonable or otherwise inadequately documented. That is the Rule of Two,
and the sustain turns on documentation. GAO did not say NIH must set the
work aside. The decision says in its own words that GAO expresses no view
on whether the agency might be able to support a reasonable decision to run
the procurement full and open. What GAO did say is that the record NIH
built gave no basis to conclude it could not expect offers from at least
two responsible small businesses, that the 2026 memorandum never explained
why three small firms found capable in 2024 no longer qualified, and that
nothing in the record showed NIH considered a partial set-aside. This is
also the second protest of NIH's effort to run this procurement full and
open. LJR's first (B-423230) was dismissed as academic on December 31, 2024
after NIH took corrective action, which tells you the agency had already
been warned once.

**Why it matters to you now.** Proposals on this RFP closed April 27, 2026,
and the tracker entry had the award pending since August. Then on August
26, 2026, twelve days after the decision, NIH cancelled the solicitation
outright (SAM.gov notice:
https://sam.gov/opp/1620f45624f243768c242f6d95ea8f8d/view). A cancellation
after a sustained terms-of-solicitation protest means the market research
gets redone and the requirement comes back as a new solicitation, on NIH's
clock, once the agency decides whether to set it aside in whole or in
part. If you are a small business with NIAID scientific, bioinformatics or
clinical research support past performance, this is the
window to make yourself visible to the contracting office: a capability
statement that answers the Rule of Two directly, with two or more comparable
small firms named if you can name them. If you are a large prime that bid
the full and open version, your teaming plan is now your hedge. A partial
set-aside is a live outcome, and the firms that already have small business
partners on paper lose the least.

**The capture lesson.** Sources sought responses are not paperwork. They are
the record GAO reads when a small business asks whether the agency looked
hard enough. When an agency publishes a sources sought and you are a small
business that can do the work, answer it, in detail, with your NAICS, your
size status and your relevant performance. When you are a large business
and the agency's set-aside determination looks thin, price the risk that a
protest reopens it. The LJR decision is the second time in this procurement
that NIH's documentation did not hold. Read the decision, map the gaps GAO
named to your own sources sought responses, and decide which side of the
Rule of Two you want to be on before the next solicitation lands.
