The sustain I want you to read this month is about a task order, not a
contract, and that is the point. On July 31, 2026, GAO sustained DAS
Federal, LLC's protest (B-424464, B-424464.2, B-424464.3) of a CMS award
under request for quotations 75FCMC26Q0023, the hosting, operation and
maintenance of the agency's integrated learning management system for the
Integrated Surveyor Training Program. CMS competed it on the GSA 8(a)
STARS III governmentwide contract, a one-year base with four one-year
options. Eleven firms quoted. CMS issued the task order to Precise Federal
Consulting, LLC on April 22, 2026, at $52,312,038. DAS, a small business
out of Gaithersburg, Maryland, had quoted $49,160,762 and lost on ratings:
Precise was rated High on corporate experience, oral presentation and
solution narrative, and DAS was rated Some on all three.

**What GAO actually found.** Two grounds carried the protest. The first was
unstated evaluation criteria. CMS marked DAS down on corporate experience
for not including a risk matrix, probability and impact scoring, mitigation
strategies specific to the program, and metrics tied to the program's own
key performance indicators. GAO read the RFQ and found it asked firms to
show how their past work demonstrated similar work. It did not ask for a
proposed solution. Grading an experience volume as if it were a technical
approach was unreasonable. The second ground was the summary narrative.
The evaluators gave DAS twelve strengths and no weaknesses, and described
five of those strengths as benefits to CMS, and then the evaluators and the
source selection authority concluded the quotation offered no benefits or
improvements. GAO called that internally inconsistent and found nothing in
the record that reconciled it. A separate challenge to the adjectival
rating was dismissed as abandoned, and DAS withdrew its arguments about the
tradeoff and the oral presentation clarifications. On prejudice, GAO
resolved the doubt in DAS's favor: a fair evaluation could have made the
lower-priced quotation competitive.

**Why it matters to you now.** GAO recommended that CMS reevaluate the
quotations, conduct and document a new best-value tradeoff, and prepare a
new source selection decision, and that it reimburse DAS its protest costs
and attorneys' fees, with the certified claim due within 60 days of the
decision. That is not a rebid. The eleven quotations that were due February
12, 2026 are the quotations CMS will reread, which means the firm that
answered the question the RFQ asked, rather than the question the
evaluators wished it had asked, is the one holding the advantage in the
redo. If you quoted on this order, pull your debrief from April 29 and
check whether your strengths and your rating tell the same story. If you
did not, watch for the new decision, because a reversal here puts a $49M
small business order on a new award date.

**The capture lesson.** Task orders on 8(a) STARS III are protestable, and
this one turned on two things you can check in any debrief. First, when an
experience volume gets dinged for missing solution content, that is an
unstated criterion, and it is a sustain ground. Second, when the strengths
in the narrative and the conclusion in the summary point in opposite
directions, the record cannot support the award. Read the RFQ's own words
for each factor, answer exactly that, and in your debrief count the
strengths against the rating. When they do not add up, you have a protest.
When they do, you have a quotation that survives one.
