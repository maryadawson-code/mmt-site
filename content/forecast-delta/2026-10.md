I re-opened every portal on this page on October 19. One agency published a
new forecast since the September read, and it is the one that matters most
for Q1 money: CMS posted its 1 October 2026 workbook, and the table below
now carries all 45 of its rows in place of September's 51. Everything else
is a story about where the forecasts live, not what is in them, because the
two browser-only tools (the GSA-hosted Forecast of Contracting Opportunities
and HHS SBCX) would not give up a fresh export to a script this month. Those
rows still carry their September 10 pull, and the table says so.

**CMS: 51 rows became 45, and the September lines are gone.** Sixteen rows
came off the forecast and ten went on. Every line that carried a September
solicitation date in the last file has disappeared: Integrated Data
Repository (September 8), Federal Services Systems Support and Oversight
(September 14), the Enterprise Privacy Policy Engine recompete (September
25), Remote Identity Proofing (September 4), PS&R/STAR (September 16) and
the NPD Ground Truth datasets line (September 1). The forecast does not say
whether each one released, awarded or got pulled, so treat them as off the
forecast and check SAM.gov before you assume anything. Also gone: Rural
Health Transformation, the Analysis and Management System for CMMI, the
expanded CJR model, the Alternative Payment Model analysis recompete, the
GLOBE and GUARD, ACCESS and GENEROUS evaluation contracts, the NAM
Leadership Consortium line, the MAO and PDP corrective action closeout, and
the One-Third Financial Audits.

The ten new rows are mostly small, with two exceptions worth your calendar.
CMS Cybersecurity Risk Management Program Support Services is a new $50M to
$100M line with a solicitation dated October 20 and award dated January 4,
2027. The Marketplace Program Integrity Contract is another $50M to $100M
line, solicitation February 26, 2027, award July 26. Behind them: a Hospital
Price Transparency Enforcement and Compliance recompete at $10M to $20M
(December 7), Payment Standardization Calculation, Application and Code
Conversion at $5M to $10M (November 18), the IPF PPS, PHP, ESRD and RNHCI
support line at $5M to $10M (November 16), an 8(a) recompete of CCIIO's
Agile Usability Experience Analysis at $5M to $10M (April 1, 2027), an
ECHIMP sole source with a June 2027 award and no solicitation date, a DME
option-year exercise at $50M to $100M for February 9, 2027, a small Open
Payment engineering support buy dated October 12, and Hospital Quality
Initiatives Public Reporting Support with an October 20 award and no
solicitation date.

Four rows moved. The Fiscal Intermediary Shared System maintainer, the
largest IT line CMS has dated, slipped from an October 2 solicitation to
October 20, and its award from December 15 to January 15, 2027. The Health
FFRDC review moved from October 15 to October 30. The Supplemental Medical
Review Contract recompete, which carried a September 11 solicitation and no
award date last month, now says December 15 with a May 10, 2027 award. And
Plan Management Compliance Review doubled its band from $20M to $50M up to
$50M to $100M with the same January 4 date. Twenty CMS lines now carry a
solicitation date inside Q1 FY27, seven carry no date at all, and four
already carry dates that have passed in a file nineteen days old: HQR
Analytics, Reporting, Business Outcomes and Research (October 5), Open
Payment engineering support (October 12), CCIIO Security and Privacy Support
(October 15) and the SourceAmerica contact center line (October 16). The
page marks those as passed. Nineteen days into the fiscal year, CMS's own
forecast is the only one in federal health that has been re-dated for FY27.

**DHA: still the FY2025 file.** The Working with DHA page links one forecast,
the FY25 Long Range Acquisition Forecast, and nothing newer. The 19 DHA rows
stay as the agency published them, with the dates that have passed marked.

**HHS: the forecast moved again, and this time there is no deep link.** The
osdbu.hhs.gov address I gave you in September now returns 404. The forecast
is still there, but it is a view inside the SBCX app at the osdbu.hhs.gov
front door, and mysbcx.hhs.gov lands on the same app. The acquisition.gov
hub links it that way too. What I could not do this month is re-pull the
export, so the IHS, FDA, NIH and Office of the Secretary rows in the table
are the September 10 read. If you need an IHS or FDA date to be current,
open the app and filter by OpDiv before you trust the table.

**VA: the query tool changed its fiscal year without changing its review
date.** VA's own Forecast of Contracting Opportunities page now says it
covers fiscal year 2027. It is the same version 26.3.0.0 with the same
reviewed stamp of May 7, 2026, so the label rolled over and the review did
not. The live rows are still in the GSA-hosted tool on acquisitiongateway.gov,
which I could confirm loads but could not re-count outside a browser. The 44
VA lines in the table are the September pull. The OSDBU acquisition page
that acquisition.gov's hub still lists for VA returned 403 again.

**GSA: one more redirect in the chain.** acquisition.gov's cross-agency hub
was updated on September 28 and now lists 22 agency links, not the four I
counted in September. Its GSA link goes to a forecast page on gsa.gov that
redirects to Find Opportunities, which points you back to the Forecast
Dashboard on acquisitiongateway.gov. Same tool, two more hops. The 12 GSA
rows are the September pull.

**ARPA-H: the open list turned over, and none of it is a forecast.** REST,
STREAM and the TIGAR exploratory topic dropped off since September. Open on
October 19: Emerging Health Innovators, FASTPASS, UNIQUE, SURPASS, SPECTRA,
ASCENT-IBO and the four rolling Mission Office ISOs. The page shows no posted
or close dates for any of them. ARPA-H has no rows this month, which is the
agency's answer, not mine.

**Two housekeeping notes.** The DoD acquisition forecast hub on
business.defense.gov answered 403 to everything I sent it on October 19,
browser agent or not; the acquisition.gov hub still lists it, so open it in
a real browser before calling it dead. And the CMS workbook URL for each
month now redirects to the newest file, so the September link you may have
bookmarked serves the October file. That is convenient and it means an old
link will never show you what CMS said last month. The table keeps the
dated source for every row.
