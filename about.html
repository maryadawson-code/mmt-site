About

The Story Behind Mission Meets Tech

Mission Meets Tech exists because federal health IT is too important — and too complex — to be covered with press releases and surface-level takes. We translate policy into practice for the people who build, buy, and operate the systems that support military and veteran healthcare.

Why This Exists

Federal health IT sits at the intersection of national security, healthcare delivery, and technology modernization. The decisions made inside DHA, VA, and Congress ripple through an ecosystem of contractors, clinicians, and service members who depend on these systems working.

Most coverage of this space is either too shallow to be useful or too dense to be actionable. Mission Meets Tech bridges that gap — delivering clear, evidence-based analysis that helps operators, executives, and policy professionals understand what's actually happening and what it means for their work.

Every piece of content passes through one filter: does this help someone save lives or enhance readiness?

Meet the Founder

Mary Womack

Founder, Mission Meets Tech

Mary has worked in federal health IT since 2007, spanning the Defense Health Agency, Veterans Affairs, and the broader Military Health System. Her experience covers the operational, technical, and strategic dimensions of how technology gets built, bought, and deployed across federal healthcare.

She launched Mission Meets Tech to create the kind of independent, evidence-based intelligence resource she wished had existed throughout her career — one that cuts through noise and delivers practical value to practitioners and decision-makers.

What We Believe

Evidence Over Opinion

Claims backed by dates, dollars, entities, and operational consequences. No speculation dressed up as analysis.

Independence Matters

Mission Meets Tech is an independent platform. Our analysis is not shaped by employer affiliation or vendor relationships.

Clarity Is Kindness

Complex policy deserves clear explanation, not jargon walls. We translate complexity into action without oversimplifying.

Mission First

Everything we produce is filtered through one question: does this help save lives or enhance readiness?
