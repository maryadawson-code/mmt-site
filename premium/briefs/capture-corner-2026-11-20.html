Confidence: High on the dated government documents (the executive order, the NIST report, the five-agency fact sheet, DHA-AI 4140.01, the Army justifications, the VA notice). Medium on the outage causes, which are owner statements with forensics still under way, and on the commissary failures, which remain a suspected and unattributed hack. Low on every agent-visibility figure as a measure of any federal agency; they are vendor surveys of commercial respondents. Analytical on every read and every owner assignment.

The lead finding for a capture team: three inventories are being demanded at once, by three different authorities, and the same class of equipment is missing from all three. Agent inventories are being asked for by CISOs after Hugging Face. Controller inventories are being asked for by CISA after the refrigeration attacks. Cryptographic inventories are required by OMB under a dated executive order. Medical devices, facility controllers, and vendor remote-access paths are the items an IT-led inventory is most likely to leave out, and they are the items the 2026 attacks reached. The offeror who can show one method that reaches those assets is describing where three requirements converge.

Three reads. First, each inventory has a different owner inside an agency: the CISO for agents, facilities and logistics for controllers, the CIO's migration lead for cryptography. Second, the vendor-access path appears in three of the four rows. Four reads. First, the definition of "covered contractors" is the open term that sizes the market, and the proposed rule is where it appears. Second, military health systems may split across two tracks. The executive order's inventory excludes National Security Systems, which follow NSA's schedule, and which DHA and Service medical systems fall on which side is a question to put to the agency. [CC20][CC24] Third, the VA notice is a sources-sought action on ten hardware security modules that sit under VA's public key infrastructure and key management, and its draft work statement asks for a post-quantum readiness assessment within 60 days of award. A follow-on solicitation is the event to watch. [CC25] Fourth, one vendor has published a roadmap titled as a DHA post-quantum transition. It is a vendor document and should not be read as DHA's plan. [CC28]

Three reads. First, the argument a reader will meet inside DHA is that the alarms exist, and the documents support that. The open questions are coverage, connectivity, and vendor access: whether every blood unit is on a separate monitor, whether "stand-alone" holds everywhere, and who can reach the monitoring system remotely. Second, the monitoring vendor's access deserves the same treatment as the refrigeration vendor's. A monitor that only its maker can update is a second supplier path into the same room. [CC15] Third, the measure that would settle it is small: the share of blood refrigerators and freezers that pass an alarm test with the controller switched off. Nothing in the public record reports that number.

5. The counterargument, stated fairly

Confidence: The strongest case against the reads above, and parts of it are right.

The alarms are already required. AABB, the FDA device classification, and DHA-AI 4140.01 all require alarms or monitoring, and the French case arguably shows current practice working. [CC18][CC13] The counter is that the attackers in Israel and in the French retail wave disabled alerts on the controllers they held, so an alarm that depends on the controller is the one that fails. [CC8]

There is no U.S. blood incident. True. No public report names a military blood bank, laboratory, or hospital refrigerator. The commissary cause is itself unconfirmed, and the Defense Commissary Agency attributes four later European failures to worn equipment and late parts. [CC7][CC29] A requirement built on analogy is a requirement that may wait.

The outage week was ordinary. Change-induced outages are as old as change windows, and a cluster can be chance. The counter is that the requirement signals in this brief do not depend on the outages being connected. Each has its own dated authority.

The FAR rule does not exist yet. The proposed rule had not been published as of late October, and "covered contractors" is undefined. [CC22] Building to a proposed rule carries risk. The executive order's agency dates are fixed regardless.

The agent numbers are marketing. Every agent-visibility figure here comes from a vendor with a product to sell. [CC4] The stronger evidence is the two companies' own accounts of the Hugging Face breach. [CC3]

Independence can be done badly. A separate monitor that pages through the same network or the same building system can be silenced with it. A requirement has to name the sensor, the power, and the notification path.

6. What to read in the next ninety days

Confidence: Analytical. These are documents to pull and questions to put in writing; none is a view on how an open requirement should be written.

  1. Pull the FAR proposed rule when it posts. Due about December 19. Read the definition of "covered contractors" and whether contractor-operated systems carry their own inventory duty. [CC20][CC22]

  2. Ask which medical systems are National Security Systems. New NSS acquisitions follow CNSA 2.0 from January 1, 2027, on NSA's track. [CC24]

  3. Watch for a VA follow-on to 36C10B26Q0474. The June notice was sources-sought. The readiness assessment at 60 days is the first deliverable to price. [CC25]

  4. Check for a revision of DHA-AI 4140.01 and a final DHA 27 52 33. An independence sentence in either one is the requirement. [CC13][CC14]

  5. Read the AABB proposed 34th edition for alarm language. [CC18]

  6. Track the commissary investigation. No forensic detail has been released. A stated cause changes the evidence weight of everything in section 2, row two. [CC7]

  7. Watch the controller advisories. Whether the six flaws reported in November get patches, and whether CISA issues a binding directive for end-of-support refrigeration controllers on federal networks. [CC12][CC11]

  8. Mark February 1, 2027. The Senate Armed Services Committee's report language directs a briefing on cybersecurity at DHA by that date, citing recent cyber incidents involving defense contractors and the integrity of network-connected medical devices. It is the nearest dated forum for the inventory question. [CC30]

  9. Mark March 1, 2027. The same report directs a review of Military Health System readiness that includes the Armed Services Blood Program. Cold storage is a fair question for it. [CC30]

  10. Mark about March 19, 2027. The second FAR rule and the cryptographic bill of materials guidance. Expect the bill of materials to appear in solicitations after it. [CC22]

  11. Follow H.R. 11046. A national investigations board for cyber and AI incidents would create a public record of root causes that does not exist today. [CC6]
