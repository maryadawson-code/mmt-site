Executive Thesis

RHRP-4 is a single-award IDIQ. That means zero prime opportunity for anyone other than OptumServe through 2036. But it also means every specialty requirement under the 4 expected task orders will be subcontracted, and the contracting activity (DHA Professional Services Contracting Division, Falls Church) is the same shop that issued RHRP-3's tasking history. Subs on RHRP-3 (LHI/OptumServe team) have a natural follow-on only if their teaming agreements carry over — confirm this week. The hidden story: population expansion to DHS civilians (CBP, TSA, FEMA readiness) is the 18-month scope-creep watch.

Subcontracting Windows — TO 2 through TO 4

Window 1 — Specialty clinical services
Behavioral health, dental, audiology, vision, lab
These are the lines where OptumServe did not have native national coverage on RHRP-3. Subs with CONUS + OCONUS footprint across these specialties will be on the shortlist for TO 2–4. Teaming agreements should be in place before TO 1 closes transition.
Window 2 — DHS civilian expansion
CBP, TSA, FEMA readiness
RHRP-4 scope was expanded to include DHS civilians. This is new ground — DHS component medical readiness has historically been handled by FEHB contractors, not DHA-style IDIQs. If OptumServe stands up a dedicated DHS task order, specialty subs with DHS past performance become differentiators.
Window 3 — Data + interoperability
MHS GENESIS integration + DHA analytics
RHRP readiness data feeds into IMR scoring for DoD deployability. As MHS GENESIS rolls out to additional sites, RHRP contractor data integration becomes a differentiator. Health IT firms with MHS GENESIS / Cerner Millennium experience should approach OptumServe's federal IT arm.
