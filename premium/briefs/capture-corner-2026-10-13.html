Confidence: High on campaign plan tasks and dates, read from the plan's LOE 1 and LOE 2 task lists. High on solicitation numbers, contract actions, and budget figures, read from SAM.gov listings, war.gov contract announcements, and the FY27 MHS justification. Medium on vendor positions, which rest on public notices, trade reporting, and one vendor white paper. Analytical on the market reads.

The lead finding for a capture team: the campaign plan turns LOE 1 into a dated buying schedule, and the first deadline is October 31. Eleven LOE 1 tasks are due October 31, 2026, most of them dashboards, algorithms, standards, and plans that DHA offices will build with the contracts and staff they already have. The new buys cluster from December through July: VIPRR acquisition options and a virtual health portfolio plan on December 31, MEND standards in January, PARATUS deliverables and private sector referral feeds in March, the deployability module and provider scorecard in June, and six virtual health pilots in July. A firm that can help DHA measure the waiting clock separately from the healing clock fits nearly every one of them.

3. MEND and the build-or-license decision

Confidence: High on the plan's MEND tasks and the commercial products. Analytical on the decision.

MEND, for Medical Evaluation and Normalization of Duration, covers the top musculoskeletal conditions, about 200, with 196 identified so far. Medical Affairs loads the first tranche into the services' readiness systems by January 31, 2027, reports lost warrior days outside standard timelines monthly from that date, delivers remediation plans by March 31, and begins quarterly effectiveness assessments by June 30. OWHA builds a provider-specific scorecard by June 30 comparing the orthopedic duty-limiting patients each provider sees with the limited-duty status each assigns. The target is 90 percent of profiles at or below the median, and the first priority pathways are ACL surgery, arthroscopic shoulder surgery, and spinal surgery. [CC1]

The commercial market already sells durations. MDGuidelines, owned by ReedGroup, which Alight acquired, publishes physiological benchmarks for uncomplicated cases with an API, and ODG by MCG builds best-case, average, and maximum durations from more than 10 million lost-time cases. [CC9][CC10][CC11] Navy Medicine wrote its own in 2021, and average musculoskeletal limited duty at Naval Medical Forces Atlantic fell from 141.97 to 101.39 days. [CC12]

Path What it looks like What it buys
License DHA licenses a commercial benchmark set and calibrates to military job demands Content licensing, calibration analytics, integration into PARATUS
Build DHA writes its own standards from service data on the Navy model Clinical informatics, profile-history data science, standards governance
Hybrid Commercial benchmarks check DHA-built standards Both, plus benchmark comparison reporting

Two reads follow from the timeline. First, 196 identified conditions and a January deadline suggest DHA is building the content in-house; licensing and calibrating a commercial set inside sixteen weeks would be fast for any agency. The open lane is calibration, benchmark comparison, and the analytics stacked on top: monthly reports, remediation tracking, quarterly assessments, and the scorecard. Second, the one remediation example the plan gives is "first available surgery date acceptance," and the surgical wait data lives in LOE 2's operating room dashboards from June 30, 2027. [CC1] Joining referral, scheduling, and surgical timestamps to the profile record separates waiting days from healing days. [CC13] No commercial duration product sells that today, and it is the most defensible analytics offer in LOE 1.

4. PARATUS and the systems it replaces

Confidence: High on the solicitation and the plan's PARATUS tasks. Analytical on the capture reads.

DHA's Contracting Activity posted Commercial Solutions Opening HT003826SC004 on July 20, 2026, for PEO Defense Healthcare Management Systems: a unified data platform to replace six service-specific legacy systems, with periodic health assessment modernization open and profiling and waivers anticipated. The architecture calls for IL5 hosting, an IL6 cross-domain solution, Unity Catalog-aligned governance, and FHIR R4, HL7 v2.5.1, SNOMED CT, ICD-10, and LOINC data contracts, with awards as other transactions under 10 U.S.C. 4022. [CC2] Trade reporting describes a six-phase evaluation built on live demonstrations, with a data-conformance gate at Phase 2. [CC14]

The plan sets the milestones: the assessment prototype award by October 31, 2026; a minimum viable product and automated private sector referral feeds into the health record and PARATUS by March 31, 2027; a deployability module delivered as a SMART on FHIR application inside MHS GENESIS, with a provider acknowledgment and disposition workflow, by June 30; and a commander view of flagged cases by September 30, 2027. The performance target is a profile action or readiness disposition on 95 percent of the deployment-limiting cases PARATUS flags. [CC1]

Legacy system Service Role today
e-Profile (in MODS) Army Profiling system of record [CC15]
MEDPROS Army Readiness data system of record [CC15]
MRRS Navy and Marine Corps Individual medical readiness tracking [CC16]
LIMDU SMART Navy and Marine Corps Limited-duty case management [CC17]
ASIMS Air Force and Space Force Individual medical readiness tracking [CC18]

Three capture reads follow. First, Unity Catalog is Databricks' governance layer, so the architecture points toward a Databricks-based platform; that is an inference, and teams should confirm it in the Q&A. [CC2] Second, MEND standards must land in the legacy readiness systems by January 31, before PARATUS can absorb them, so the transition work is a market of its own. The Army's MODS services ran on IDIQ W81K0419D0007, awarded to Tiber Creek Consulting in 2019, and MEDCOM's FY24 forecast listed a $40 million small business recompete through 2029; confirm the current holder. [CC19][CC20] Third, the March referral feeds put network specialty waits into the same platform as the profile, and reserve health assessments arrive through OptumServe's RHRP-4 contract, so the data contracts need to take both or the count has holes. [CC21]

5. The virtual health buildout

Confidence: High on the plan's virtual health tasks. Medium on vendor positions, which rest partly on a vendor white paper. Analytical on the reads.

The plan's virtual health objective calls for more synchronous and asynchronous capacity and a 5 percent increase in primary care appointments done virtually. Beyond the dated tasks in Section 2, it ties the nurse advice line and VIPRR to a 10 percent rise in use and a 10 percent drop in private sector urgent care visits, adds text and chat for virtual appointments in the spring, and calls for an analysis of legacy virtual health capabilities and workforce by September 30, 2027. The portfolio plan due December 31 must reconfigure legacy programs for HL7 FHIR compliance and interoperability "across platforms and commercial partners." [CC1]

Amwell has already mapped itself to this list. Its white paper on the FY27 effort walks each task and pitches its platform as the common virtual care layer for the portfolio plan. It reports about 400,000 annual virtual visits in the DHA environment against about 124,000 in the legacy environment, 98 percent technical success, 92 percent provider and 91 percent patient satisfaction, and more than 99.9 percent uptime for My Military Health visits. It says VIPRR already runs on My Military Health scheduled virtual visits, and it names Sword Health as its partner for the orthopedic virtual consults. [CC22] Sword holds the virtual physical therapy pilot on a sole-source extension, and Amwell said in August that negotiations on its direct DHA contract were ongoing. [CC6][CC23] The figures are company-reported. DHA's own BRAVE program, run by its Virtual Medical Center, cut the first mental health appointment wait at Misawa from 32 days to 5. [CC24]

My read is that the orthopedic consults and VIPRR capacity favor the platform in place, and Amwell and Sword have arrived as a pair. The open lanes for everyone else are the portfolio plan itself, through FHIR interoperability and legacy program rationalization, the six pilots due in July, the legacy analysis due in September 2027, and outcome measurement, which no one has published. A firm that can show waiting days saved per injured service member, measured independently of the platform vendor, has an offer DHA cannot get from its incumbents.

7. Prevention and public health lines

Confidence: High on the plan's targets and the cited baselines. Analytical on the market.

Injury cause coding. The plan raises cause coding from 10 percent of new injury encounters to 50 percent within a year, with a gap analysis due October 31, 2026 and policy and training due September 30, 2027. [CC1] Only 10 percent of 5.97 million injury encounters from 2016 through 2019 carried a cause code, and overuse injuries less than 2 percent of the time. [CC29] With ambient documentation now enterprise-wide and Solventum named for coding, my read is that prompting for mechanism in ambient notes, with software suggesting the code, is the fastest route to 50 percent, with audits for accuracy. [CC30][CC3]

Hearing. The plan adds capacity for 408,000 hearing surveillance exams and 350,000 fit tests. Boothless audiometry courses of action are due January 30, 2027, and procurement of NIOSH-compliant equipment, boothless audiometers named, with a Phase 1 rollout to hospitals on Air Force installations and Navy bases with mobile hearing conservation trucks, is due September 30, 2027. [CC1] That is the clearest equipment buy in LOE 1. The force tested 1,355,246 members in 2023, and the Army's 169 boothless units matched booth thresholds across about 12,000 exams; one technician still cannot monitor several boothless tests at once, and that limitation is the opening. [CC31][CC32]

Blast overpressure. A consolidated exposure and brain health assessment is due December 31, 2026, and a risk management plan with resources through PPBE by March 31, 2027. [CC1] GAO found resource constraints impeded cognitive assessments and exposure tracking, and DHA's monitoring tool covers all 15 Tier 1 weapons systems. [CC33][CC34]

Health promotion and embedded care. Public Health owes a clinical health promotion scan and core operating model with resource requirements by January 31, 2027, aimed at a 5 to 10 percent cut in musculoskeletal injury risk through waist circumference, body composition, BMI, prior injury, and profile status. [CC1] Army H2F carries $325.6 million in FY27 Regular Army operation and maintenance funds, and a 2026 study tied H2F brigades to 5.9 percent fewer limited-duty workdays after musculoskeletal injury; the July 1 Capture Corner mapped the recompete. [CC35][CC36][CC37]

8. The counterargument, stated fairly

Confidence: This is the strongest case against the reads above, and parts of it are right.

Dates move. The plan builds in quarterly updates that can change tasks, and most October 31 items are internal builds that can slip without any contract action. Treat the schedule as a sequence more than a calendar.

Transitions can be smooth. DHA's sole-source notice reports that no respondent recommended replacing the core components, and direct contracts with existing vendors may change the paper more than the service. [CC3] The re-contracting risk is real for the referral center, scheduling, and virtual PT, and smaller for telehealth.

Virtual care evidence is thin. DHA has published no outcome numbers for the virtual PT pilot, and the strongest figures in circulation come from the platform vendor. [CC28][CC22] Teams selling virtual musculoskeletal care should bring their own outcome measures, defined in waiting days and healing days.

Standards may shrink some markets. If MEND shortens profiles, demand for services tied to long profiles falls. That is the policy working, and it argues for selling measurement and prevention over volume.

The architecture inference may be wrong. Unity Catalog alignment does not guarantee a Databricks platform.

9. What to do in the next ninety days

Confidence: Analytical.

  1. Map your offer to the October 31 tasks and sell to the lead office. Most are internal builds by Healthcare Operations, Medical Affairs, and OWHA, supported through vehicles those offices already use. [CC1]

  2. Get in front of the VIPRR decision before December 31. Bring capacity models and outcome measures, and leave platform features to the incumbent. [CC1]

  3. Shape the virtual health portfolio plan. FHIR interoperability, legacy program rationalization, and the July pilots are the open lanes. [CC1][CC22]

  4. Read the PARATUS CSO for the profiling area of interest before it posts. Map your data model to the listed standards, the six legacy exports, and the March referral feeds. [CC2][CC14][CC1]

  5. Build a waiting-clock demonstration on the ACL pathway. Join synthetic referral, appointment, and surgery timestamps to a synthetic profile and show healing and waiting days separately. It answers the plan's own remediation example and fits a live-demo evaluation. [CC13][CC1]

  6. Sell MEND support as calibration and analytics. Benchmark comparison and scorecard analytics fit a standard DHA appears to be writing itself. [CC9][CC11][CC12]

  7. Track the equipment and scheduling buys. Hearing equipment by September 30, 2027 and the AI scheduling acquisition strategy by March 30, 2027 are the written signals of new money in and around LOE 1. [CC1][CC32]
