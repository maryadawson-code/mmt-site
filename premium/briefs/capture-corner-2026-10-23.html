Everything that touches patient entry runs toward whatever HCO writes into the RC3 requirement this winter.

Protests A pre-award protest, B-423625.1, filed June 10, 2025, dismissed June 30, 2025; a second protest, B-423625.2, filed November 13, 2025, dismissed February 10, 2026 [CC3]
Policy DHA-PI 6025.26 (October 18, 2019): the NAL is a designated PCM for 24/7 advice, books only 24-hour appointments after RN triage, and MTFs "shall not refer or transfer beneficiaries who need an appointment to the NAL"; ten-year expiration [CC13]
Volume Over 530,000 callers a year, about 1,400 a day; 82 percent MTF enrollees; satisfaction above 91 percent in the FY24 evaluation and 94 percent as spoken at the June industry day [CC14][CC15][CC4]
Plan tasks Nurse line and VIPRR utilization up 10 percent, private sector urgent care down 10 percent, due December 31, 2026 (ATC-2(4)); VIPRR primary care capacity acquisition options, December 31 (ATC-4(1)(b)) [CC1]

Three reads. First, the timeline fits corrective action: a protest filed two weeks after proposals were due and dismissed in February, followed by a June bridge, is the shape of an agency taking an award back for a second look. That is inference; the docket names no awardee and no reason. Second, the plan has pre-committed the line's growth before its owner is settled, so whoever holds the line on January 1 inherits a 10 percent push with no transition period. Third, the 2019 instruction is the real scope boundary. As written it caps the line at same-day booking; the public issue's fix is to make the line the front router with warm transfer to the appointment center, VIPRR, scheduled virtual visits, Quick Care Connect, and network urgent care. A GNAL-NG offeror that can show booking and warm-transfer integration beyond 24 hours is pricing the next requirement, not this one.

One caution for teams reading the public issue: the TRICARE Prime demonstration in Atlanta and Tampa runs its own 24/7 nurse line through its contractor, its enrollees cannot use the MHS line, and about 150,000 beneficiaries are eligible, so the nurse line's footprint is already short of enterprise-wide. [CC16][CC17]

4. The enterprise appointing center

Confidence: High on the public record, which is unusually complete. The transcript is machine-generated; quote only after checking the recording.

Status. DHA's own answer: "we are currently in the early data-gathering phase of our acquisition strategy," operating under DoDI 5000.74, "specifically in Step 3 (Market Research)," with industry input feeding "a structured Analysis of Alternatives (AoA)" for leadership. The contracting officer anticipated an RFP in the second quarter of FY2027 and an award in the first quarter of FY2028, with further industry days, RFIs, and possibly a draft RFP first. The DHA Office of Small Business Programs sits on the integrated product team reviewing "potential bundling concerns." [CC4]

The requirement as described. The special notice calls today's appointing "fragmented across over 110 separate contracts and call centers" and describes a "centralized system for all patient appointments." [CC18] The requirements owner, DHA's deputy assistant director for Healthcare Operations and the former chief of the division that owns the nurse line, said the solution "can be one, it can be multiple. We're not sure," framed it as air traffic control, said patients and staff should not have to relearn the system every PCS, and tied the center to LOE 1 (limited-duty days), LOE 2 (surgical volume and complexity at the Core 15), and LOE 4. The routing logic she described is "keep care in that we need and bring care in that we also need," complexity to the Core 15, low-complexity care to "another alternative." [CC4]

Who was in the room. Firms visible in the transcript's attendee labels or by self-identification include the nurse line incumbent, the West region managed care contractor, and several platform and services firms; most attendees were silent and many appear by personal name only. [CC4] Partners the requirements owner named: VA, a large civilian integrated health system, and the managed care contractors. The nature of the civilian partnership is unverified.

Non-answers. DHA grouped and did not answer industry's questions about current contracts and about pending legislation that could change requirements. The legislation is the FY27 NDAA (section 8). [CC4]

Reads. The requirement is a router with a case-mix rule inside it: defer when the network is faster, recapture when the Core 15 needs the case. Both can be true, but the logic has to hold in both directions, and the plan's deferral guidance (December 31) will be written before the RFP. A team that can show availability visibility across sites, a recapture engine, and a transition plan from 110 incumbents is answering the three hardest questions. The small business pressure point is bundling; watch for a set-aside or partial carve-out decision before the RFP.

Related. IRMAC, the Indo-Pacific regional appointing solicitation (HT941026R2001, 8(a), amended August 2026), is the regional version already in procurement, and Colorado Springs and Eglin run RC3s the plan calls mature. [CC20][CC1] A regional award before the enterprise RFP is a reference case for the enterprise buy.

9. The counterargument, stated fairly

Confidence: The strongest case against the reads above, and parts of it are right.

DHA is building the one door, just not in this document. The RC3 notice, the industry day, the February report's "centralized contact centers," IRMAC, and the mature RC3s at Colorado Springs and Eglin are all real. [CC18][CC4][CC15][CC20][CC1] The campaign plan is DHA's FY27 execution document; it dates the doors without dating the convergence, and the convergence is FY28 at the earliest. Both statements are true.

Multiple doors are a feature. Active duty, Prime families, network enrollees, and demonstration enrollees need different channels, and commercial systems run many channels behind one router. The router is the missing piece, not the channels, and the requirements owner says on the record that the router may be one or several. [CC4]

Access is already in standard. The February report measured specialty wait at 18.0 days against 28 and recommended no change to the standards. [CC15] Averages hide entry friction, and the plan's own nurse line and urgent care targets only make sense if beneficiaries are leaving through the wrong door.

VA's door is not clean. VA's inspector general found about 573,000 of 2.9 million calls to sampled clinics went to wrong numbers and about 530,000 to voicemail, and hub physicians were not consistently credited for spoke work. [CC52][CC53] Use VA as a construct, not a model of perfection.

A door does not create supply. DHA's own line to Congress: telemedicine "does not increase provider capacity." [CC15] That is why the staffing analysis due December 31 and the credit rules matter more than the router, and why the Defense Health Board's 2023 finding on RVU credit for virtual care, with no task in the plan, is the gap a serious offer names. [CC54][CC1]

Counter to the counter. Three procurements are live or dated, the five direct contracts have a start date, and the deferral guidance, the directory feedback task, and the staffing analysis all land by December 31. The door market moves this winter whether or not the center is awarded.

10. What to do in the next ninety days

Confidence: Analytical.

  1. Resolve the nurse line question first. Check the GAO docket for B-423625 corrective action and watch for a GNAL-NG award or a second bridge before December 17. Price any triage, booking, or warm-transfer capability to the next requirement, booking beyond 24 hours, and treat the 2019 instruction's ceiling as the thing that requirement replaces. [CC3][CC13]

  2. Answer DHA's eleven RC3 questions in writing before the next RFI. Questions 3, 5, and 6 (real-time availability, recapture, and many-to-one transition) are the discriminators. The June window closed; the next engagement is where the paper goes. [CC4]

  3. Watch the bundling decision. The small business office is on the IPT. A set-aside or carve-out before the RFP changes the teaming map for 110 incumbents. [CC4]

  4. Treat the deferral guidance due December 31 as a draft requirement. The routing logic it writes will be in the RFP; teams with network access analytics and recapture determination tools should read it the day it posts. [CC1]

  5. Position for the beneficiary digital experience pillar and the EHR and virtual care platform integration seams. The direct contracts set the platforms by July 2027; integration, adoption, and the Pillar 8 AoI are the competitive openings. [CC5][CC26]

  6. Sell the quality half where the money is. Sterile processing, self-collection kits and lab throughput, NSQIP reviewers and champions, and ambient AI adoption have dates between November 30 and July 2027 and named owners. [CC29][CC32][CC14][CC38]

  7. Map the NDAA sections to the pipeline. Section 701, the directory accuracy section, and the material-failure notice would create requirements the plan does not; a lame-duck enactment moves them from watch items to capture items. [CC47][CC49]

  8. Hold the OHI and claims lane for the THP dashboard. March 31, 2027 is dated, and the West-region problem has state and press attention. [CC40][CC1]
