Confidence: High on the dated government documents (the campaign order, the budget request, the two inspector general reports, the DHA announcement and notice). Medium on the hearing record, which is taken from press coverage and one written statement with transcripts not reviewed, and on the VA contract holders, which rest on a secondary source. Low on every vendor-reported result and on the investor and payer surveys as measures of any federal program. Analytical on every read and every owner assignment.

The lead finding for a capture team: the note has been bought. DHA's comes from its record vendor and VA's sits on a five-year vehicle, so the next federal dollars attach to what surrounds it: adoption support against a dated 67 percent target, the measures that would show a return, the safety reporting VA's inspector general asked for, the controls on each feature that arrives after the note, the analytics a payer needs on the outside care both departments buy, and the phone lines. Each of those has a named owner or a dated document in the public record. The scribe license is the piece with the least left to compete for.

4. The controls, with a measure for each

Confidence: High on what each vendor or panel said. Analytical on the requirement column and on the measures. The requirement column describes what the public record shows vendors and buyers discussing, and it takes no position on any open solicitation.

Control What was described What it becomes in a requirement A measure
Human review The deploying organization decides which steps need a person's approval [CC11] Approval gates by class of action: draft, order, submit Share of agent actions carrying a logged human approval
Audit log Every action logged [CC11] A step-level log, retained with the record and exportable to the government Share of actions that can be reconstructed from the log
Permission limits A runtime that scopes what an agent can reach; read access only for outside agents at Epic [CC22][CC11] A permitted-action list with write actions off by default Count of actions attempted outside the list
Shadow mode The agent shows what it would do before it is allowed to act [CC22] A pre-production period with an acceptance threshold Agreement rate between proposed actions and clinician actions
Short terms and refunds A year or less with benchmarks; refunds against agreed metrics [CC17] Task-order milestones tied to measured outcomes Baseline against six-month results
Data terms Raised at the House hearing; Oracle said it has no access to veterans' data [CC9] Explicit limits on vendor use of patient data, model training included Contract language, verified at award

Two reads. First, these six lines are the vendors' own vocabulary, which lowers the cost of asking for them. A requirement that names approval gates and action logs is asking for what OpenAI, Epic, and NVIDIA have already described in public. Second, the data row is the newest. A member of the House committee said Oracle's sales team had pressed for commercial data-privacy terms, Oracle's executive answered that the company has no access to veterans' data, and the committee's ranking member tied the question to what that data is worth now that it can train models. [CC9] Expect data-use language to draw more attention in AI buys on both sides of the federal record.

5. The counterargument, stated fairly

Confidence: The strongest case against the reads above, and parts of it are right.

Much of this is announcement. Ergo Visit is at three organizations, the Ambience prior authorization workflow was a demonstration, Oracle's prior authorization agent is on a roadmap, the Tennr pilot has no results, and by one trade account much of Suki's agent work is planned. [CC20][CC11][CC10][CC17] A requirement written to an announcement can wait a long time for a product.

The record vendor will absorb it. Incumbents took 68 percent of new scribe dollars, and DHA's next features arrive as Oracle releases. [CC1][CC10] If the pattern holds for orders, coding, and prior authorization, the third-party opening narrows to services and measurement. The counter is that VA chose a multi-vendor vehicle for the note, and that adoption, safety reporting, and payer analytics sit outside any record vendor's license.

Pricing is not public. Oracle's co-CEO told senators that VA's price includes access to clinical AI. That line is a reporter's paraphrase of testimony about VA, and it says nothing about what any other customer pays. [CC9] What DHA pays for the Clinical AI Agent does not appear in the public record reviewed for this brief. The FY2027 request shows only that ambient listening implementation is funded inside a $200.4 million increase. [CC5] A price-to-win built on either number alone is a guess.

Costs are rising before they fall. Google Cloud told the conference that unit costs are dropping while total use is "exploding," Elevance expects costs up in every market in the short term, and about 40 percent of health executives in one survey paused AI projects over unpredictable costs. [CC23] Consumption pricing inside a fixed-price task order moves that risk to the offeror.

Higher codes may be accurate codes. The Wisconsin trial found billing compliance held, and the study behind the coding concern had no control group. [CC2][CC18] Payer analytics that start from an assumption of inflation will be wrong some of the time.

The evidence is a version behind. The trials tested 2024 and 2025 products. [CC2] Measures written today may describe a product that has already changed.

6. What to read in the next ninety days

Confidence: Analytical. These are documents to pull and questions to put in writing; none is a view on how an open requirement should be written.

  1. Pull the final HIPAA access rule from the Federal Register. Confirm the effective and compliance dates, and read what a qualifying personal health application has to do once it holds the record. [CC16]

  2. Pull the proposed CRUSH rule. Comments are due January 19, 2027. Read the referral mandate and the enrollment reporting for what they require of plans' data systems. [CC15]

  3. Watch DHA's December 31 deliverables. The published best practices and the engagement strategy are the nearest documents to a statement of what DHA will measure. [CC4]

  4. Check the status of the first area of interest under HT003826SC005. Adoption and change support is the scope closest to the 67 percent target. [CC6]

  5. Ask which Clinical AI Agent features are enabled at DHA. Order creation and coding have shipped commercially. Whether either is on in military hospitals is not in the public record reviewed. [CC10]

  6. Watch appropriations after December 11. The digital health increase is a request until it is enacted. [CC5]

  7. Read task orders under VA's ambient contract as they post. Look for outcome measures and for scope beyond the note. [CC7]

  8. Check whether inspector general recommendation 3 has closed. A live AI event category in VA patient safety reporting is the marker. [CC8]

  9. Follow the hearing record. GAO is still waiting on the analysis behind VA's cost estimate, and the data-terms exchange has no written follow-up in the coverage reviewed. [CC9]

  10. Watch for the next action on DHA's Enterprise Referral Care Coordination Center notice. It is the appointing document on the public record. [CC14]

  11. Mark the first quarter of 2027. Epic's outside-agent access and its March marketplace will show what read and write permission review looks like in practice. [CC11]

  12. Watch the state testing programs CMS pointed to. They are where the line between assisting a clinician and making a licensed decision gets drawn first. [CC15]
