The lead finding for a capture team: LOE 3 is a requirements year with a handful of fielding events, and the fielding events are where the FY27 money is. Blood, the medical examiner, and the Role 1 care platform get delivered in FY27; the rest of the line produces plans, submissions, and agreements that become FY29 money at the earliest. The blood line is the clearest buy because the budget names its own bottleneck, people, and because the plan dates every step from a Guam protocol on October 31 to European donor testing in September 2027. The trauma registry and the planning toolkit are the two active re-competes. Everything that touches the common operating picture runs toward Maven.

3. The blood market

Confidence: High on the budget language and the licensed product. Medium on vendor positions. Analytical on the reads.

The $42.5 million names three purposes: freeze-dried plasma, pre-deployment donor screening, and staffing and modernization across 20 donor centers, and the justification ties the increase to "understaffing in blood collection and insufficient funding." [CC2] Each purpose has a market.

Two reads follow. First, the demand side is public enough to size an offer. A 2026 Transfusion paper cites a planning estimate of about 10,080 low titer O whole blood units a month for a large-scale fight, against donor centers that collect in the hundreds a month, and whole blood keeps 21 to 35 days. [CC13][CC14] A capture team in this market is selling throughput: donors screened per day, units collected per hour, units tested per day, and days from collection to a forward shelf, and the Joint Base Charleston drive that collected about 40 units an hour is the kind of number DHA will want for every Core 15 hospital. [CC15] Second, the regulatory ceiling shapes the market. Walking blood bank whole blood is not FDA approved, federal rules limit release before testing to documented emergencies, and theater policy puts cold-stored tested blood first. [CC16][CC17][CC18] The money follows tested, licensed, shelf-stable products and the people who collect them.

Three reads. First, the registry re-compete is the contract vehicle behind the plan's 100 percent capture task, and a bridge through March 2027 means the award window and the task deadline sit three months apart; watch for the solicitation this winter. [CC3] Second, the planning RFI asks for stochastic consumption and stockout modeling for blood and materiel, which is the same math the 45 to 90 days of supply requirements need, so one capability answers two efforts. [CC19] Third, the hardest part of the data spine is the cross-domain path from unclassified to secret to top secret, where accredited solutions are scarce and slow to approve, and the plan's SIPR outage roadmap is an admission that the path breaks today. A question for the summit is who owns MEDHUB sustainment now that its developing command is gone. [CC23]

5. Fatality management

Confidence: High on the budget line and the plan's tasks. Analytical on the interface read.

The medical examiner system gets $42.1 million in FY27, which the justification ties to extending examiner reach through technology. [CC2] The plan sequences the work: implement the decision memorandum on fragmented remains by December 18, update large-scale combat fatality policy by March 30, deliver a new medical examiner operating system under PAE Medical Digital Solutions by June 27, finish a surge staffing plan and install upgraded CT scanners by June 30, and digitize the Armed Forces Repository of Specimen Samples for the Identification of Remains by August 30, 2027. [CC1] The Army fielded MARTS-V2 in July, which lets mortuary affairs specialists document remains so the examiner can process evidence remotely and establish identification in country. [CC26] The Army tool is the edge and the DHA operating system is the core, and the interface between them is undefined in public. It is the least covered effort in LOE 3, it has the only "install" verb in the line, and it is the one most likely to be bought on schedule.

6. Logistics, in brief

Confidence: High on the records cited. Full treatment reserved for a later Capture Corner.

The plan moves the Theater Lead Agent for Medical Materiel Pacific from DLA to DHA by September 30, 2027, with a proposal by December 31, a DLA agreement extending the Defense Working Capital Fund and a LOGICOLE rollout plan by March 31, and an allowance standard list by June 30. [CC1] TLAMM-Pacific was established in 2009 and runs through the Air Force's 18th Medical Group at Kadena as DLA's extension into the theater, and in 2022 and 2023 Army Reserve units bypassed it, ordering supplies stateside and hand-carrying them to Pacific exercises. [CC27] The vehicles underneath include the $42 million LOGICOLE continuous service improvement award to Ellumen and partners, the DML-ES task order GS35F0441Y-HT001124F0053, ITC's $36 million medical logistics IT program office contract, DLA's Medical/Surgical Prime Vendor Generation VI solicitation SPE2DV25R0001, and ADS's October addition to the DLA medical surge contract at a $450 million ceiling. [CC28][CC29][CC30][CC31][CC32] RAND warns that forward stock carries storage, monitoring, expiration, and transport costs and that scaling current approaches "may not be sufficient." [CC33] That brief will work the redesignation, the catalog, and the surge contracts together.

7. The counterargument, stated fairly

Confidence: This is the strongest case against the reads above, and parts of it are right.

Paper is the point of FY27. Requirements have to be codified before resources follow, and a PPBE submission made in FY27 is funded no earlier than FY29. On that reading, the North Star percentages describe intent, and the plan says so with its own "by 2028." Capture teams should not price FY27 as a delivery year outside blood, the examiner, and Role 1.

The people pool is shared. Donor center staff, trauma augmentation teams, the forward element, the joint operations center, surge staffing above manning, and METC faculty all draw on a uniformed medical force GAO found down about 16 percent from FY2015 to FY2023. [CC34] Every staffing-dependent task in LOE 3 competes with the Core 15 staffing problem in the LOE 2 brief.

The regulatory ceiling is real. Federal rules, Navy policy, and theater policy all make the walking blood bank a last resort. [CC17][CC35][CC18] A vendor pitching routine field collection is pitching against policy.

Maven concentration is a risk and a moat. One platform for the operating picture, public health dashboards, and theater reporting raises dependency questions, and it sends the integration work to firms already inside that environment. [CC4]

The civilian backstop is unproven. The Military-Civilian Medical Surge Program has no published findings, and the academic case says civilian hospitals are "not structured to absorb a sustained influx." [CC36][CC37]

Counter to the counter. The blood, trauma, and examiner money is already in the FY27 request, and freeze-dried plasma procurement, the Role 1 web stage, CT scanners, FASTRAK, and the Yokota satellite are all FY27 events. [CC2][CC1]

8. What to do in the next ninety days

Confidence: Analytical.

  1. Sell throughput to the donor centers, with the October 31 validations as the demand signal. Price phlebotomy staffing, screening workflow, and testing turnaround as units per day against the budget's own understaffing language, and treat the Red Cross partnership as the competitor for surge collection. [CC2][CC12]

  2. Get ahead of the FASTRAK question. Ask DHA whether it is a product, a build, or a process, and bring a pre-deployment screening workflow that implements DoDI 6480.04 end to end, including titers. [CC1][CC9]

  3. Position for the registry solicitation this winter. The incumbent's bridge ends March 31, 2027, and the capture task is due June 30. Cloud migration, data conformance, and the integrated interface are the three scoring areas to expect. [CC3][CC1]

  4. Answer the planning RFI's math for two efforts at once. Stochastic blood and materiel consumption modeling serves the planning toolkit MVP in March and the days of supply requirements due from November through February. [CC19][CC1]

  5. Bring the equipment list a quote. The JTS walking blood bank equipment list lands March 31; collection kits, rapid tests, and cold chain sized to the three roster sites are the first order. [CC1]

  6. Map the examiner's three buys. The operating system, the CT scanners, and the repository digitization land between June and August 2027, and the Army edge tool needs an interface. [CC1][CC26]

  7. Treat the Maven contingency contract as the entry point to the operating picture. The plan names it for April 30, and the common picture across three networks follows in September; integration and theater reporting support inside that environment is the lane. [CC1][CC4]

  8. Hold the logistics chase for the next brief. The DLA agreement and LOGICOLE plan in March are the first dated signals, and they get their own Capture Corner.
