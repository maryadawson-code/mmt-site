1. The picture at a glance

Field Detail
The desk A DD Form 2870 request is filled by a processor who checks MHS GENESIS, AHLTA, Essentris, HAIMS, Paper Record Tracking, and the Joint Longitudinal Viewer, then reviews and redacts every page; GAO calls redaction "the most cumbersome part," one facility reported about six days for a 10,000-page record, and one official estimated the patient portal holds about 40 percent of the record [CC1]
Scale 9.5 million beneficiaries, 547 facilities; 133 facilities reported release data and GAO could not confirm its reliability; two of four visited facilities reported staffing shortfalls with hiring freezes limiting backfill [CC1]
Standards in force DHA-PM 6025.02: 30 business days, copies free to the patient, escalation to the MTF Director at the tenth working day; posted facility standards from 20 duty days to 30 to 60 business days; HIPAA Right of Access: 30 calendar days plus one 30-day extension [CC2][CC3][CC4]
The automated path VBMS requests service treatment records from HAIMS at claim establishment for service ending on or after January 1, 2014; AHLTA Web Print uploads through HAIMS; the joint HIE reaches more than 90 percent of U.S. hospitals [CC1][CC5][CC6]
The archive DHA's archiving vendor reports CHCS archived at 26 facilities and Essentris at 31 with AHLTA in progress; DHA's February privacy assessment says AHLTA capabilities are "expected to be fully subsumed by MHS GENESIS" no later than October 2026; a Legacy Data Consolidation Solution for health information assets has been through an RFI under the Enterprise Intelligence and Data Solutions program [CC7][CC8][CC9]
The recommendation GAO-27-107784, Recommendation 5: ASD(HA) with the DHA Director to assess the records processing framework inside the health informatics business-function study, "such as consolidating databases or using artificial intelligence"; DOD concurred; the parent business-function study had a March 31, 2026 target with no reported update as of April [CC1][CC10]
The dates EO 14426: records to VA at discharge effective October 8; IT contract interoperability review due about January 6, 2027; systems and policy due about March 7, 2027. FY27 NDAA: House Sec. 761 personal health record pilot; Senate Sec. 721 secure access prototype; both in lame-duck reconciliation [CC11][CC12]

Confidence: High on the GAO findings, the manual, the executive order, and the facility pages. Medium on the archive status, which rests on a vendor statement and a privacy assessment's forward-looking language. Low on whether AHLTA was absorbed on schedule and whether single-patient export is enabled in GENESIS; both are open checks. Analytical on the market reads.

The lead finding for a capture team: the records desk is the first place the health informatics study can show a result, and the buy underneath it is three lanes, not one. The archive lane is the legacy consolidation work already in motion under EIDS, and the question that decides its value for release of information is whether a processor can search it. The tooling lane is assisted redaction and secure delivery, where HHS and State have already run pilots and VA has done market research for an enterprise FOIA and eDiscovery platform. The measurement lane is the cheapest and the least contested: the day-10 escalations and the PHI Management Tool disclosure log already generate turnaround data at every facility, and the agency that rolls it up first sets the metric everyone else is held to. The executive order funds attention on the government-to-government path through March. The person's path is where the requirement has not been written, and a team that writes it in the language of GAO's fifth recommendation is answering a question DOD has already agreed to ask.

Three reads. First, the archive row and the redaction row are the two with real technology spend behind them, and the archive row is already a program; the redaction row is a pilot waiting for the assessment GAO's fifth recommendation calls for. Second, the measurement row costs almost nothing and sets the terms for every other row, because once DHA publishes a turnaround number, every vendor's claim gets priced against it. Third, the intake and delivery rows sit on a platform DHA already licenses, which makes them configuration and workflow work on the Oracle side and a question of what the Solution Delivery Division has turned on.

3. The archive lane

Confidence: Medium. The vendor statement and the RFI summary are secondary; the privacy assessment is primary but forward-looking.

Item Record
Legacy archiving to date DHA's archiving vendor states that CHCS has been archived at 26 facilities and Essentris at 31, with AHLTA in progress [CC7]
AHLTA DHA privacy impact assessment, February 19, 2026: "AHLTA capabilities are expected to be fully subsumed by MHS GENESIS, DoD's modernized EHR, NLT October 2026." AHLTA is listed as owned and managed by the Solution Delivery Division under PEO Medical Systems (J-6) [CC8]
Consolidation A Legacy Data Consolidation Solution for health information assets (LDCS-HIA) has been through an RFI under the Enterprise Intelligence and Data Solutions program, per an aggregator summary; verify the notice on SAM.gov for the current status and any follow-on [CC9]
Retirement office The Legacy Evolutionary Analytics office within the Solution Delivery Division retires systems no longer in use [CC7]
Release-of-information view GAO found processors still accessing AHLTA, Essentris, and HAIMS to assemble records as of its fieldwork; whether the archive is exposed to records processors is not stated in any public document reviewed for this brief [CC1]

Three reads. First, the archive is being built for analytics and legal retention, and the records desk is a use case it may not have been scoped for. A team working the EIDS program should ask one question in the next industry exchange: can a release-of-information processor search the archive by patient, by date range, and by document type, and export the result with provenance. If the answer is yes, the six-system problem is mostly solved by work already under contract. If the answer is no, the gap is a requirement the EIDS program has yet to write. Second, the AHLTA date is the hinge. If AHLTA's capabilities were absorbed on the October target, processor logins to AHLTA should be retired now and the request path shortens by one system this quarter. If the date slipped, the slip is itself a finding worth tracking, because GAO's fieldwork describes processors using AHLTA and the privacy assessment describes AHLTA going away. Third, the JLV row deserves a separate read: the viewer exists to show a clinician a combined view, and a records desk that exports from JLV is exporting a view. A defensible release comes from the source system or the archive.

4. The tooling lane: redaction and delivery

Confidence: High on the federal precedents as published. Medium on the VA market research, which rests on an aggregator summary. Analytical on the requirements reads.

Precedent What it did What it shows
HHS, 2026 Chief FOIA Officer Report AI and large-language-model assisted review and redaction, with human review before release [CC15] A sister department running the pilot DHA would need
State Department pilot, reported to the FOIA Advisory Committee, 2023 Tools handled names, Social Security numbers, and email addresses well; struggled with judgment-based exemptions [CC16] Scope to identifiers, which is most of a health record's redaction load
VA enterprise FOIA and eDiscovery market research AI-assisted PII and PHI redaction subject to human review, per the aggregator summary [CC17] Requirements language a DHA solicitation could borrow, on the same platform family
HHS OCR Right of Access corrective action plans Logs of request dates and completion dates required of covered entities [CC18] The turnaround metric design, enforced on the private sector today
NARA NPRC backlog recovery More than 600,000 requests cleared through digitization, added staff, contract labor, and a VA partnership; most routine requests under a week [CC19] A surge model and a published service level

Four reads. First, the assessment GAO asked for is the acquisition gate. Recommendation 5 asks for an assessment inside the informatics study that considers consolidation and AI; the pilot follows the assessment, and a team that helps DHA write the assessment is positioned for the pilot. Second, the human-in-the-loop requirement is settled across every precedent, and a solution narrative that leads with autonomy will lose to one that leads with review throughput and audit. Third, OCR is the quiet requirement. GAO's finding is specific to large and scanned files; a redaction tool that reads native GENESIS output and fails on a scanned 2009 paper chart addresses the easy half. Fourth, delivery is a privacy control. GAO's tally of the Navy's improper releases traces most of them to unsecured email, which means a secure delivery channel reduces the incident count the agency reports to Congress, and that is a measurable outcome a proposal can name.

5. The platform lane: export, intake, and the portal

Confidence: High on Oracle's certification listing. Low on what DHA has enabled; this is the open check that matters most for the platform lane.

Oracle Health's certified health IT listing includes an electronic health information export module and FHIR-based application programming interfaces, which the federal certification program requires of the platform. [CC20] Whether DHA has enabled single-patient EHI export inside MHS GENESIS, and whether records processors use it, is not stated in any public DHA document reviewed for this brief. The patient portal is the other half: facility pages note that pre-GENESIS encounters are not in it, and one official's estimate to GAO put the portal at about 40 percent of the record. [CC1][CC13] VA's own request path runs through secure messaging with a three-business-day acknowledgment, on the same platform family. [CC4]

Three reads. First, if EHI export is enabled, the desk's work for post-go-live records becomes an export plus a redaction pass, and the processor's six-system search collapses to the legacy years. If it is not, the configuration question goes to the Solution Delivery Division and the DHMSM program office, and it is a question about a capability DHA already licenses. Second, intake through the portal's secure messaging is a workflow configuration VA has already shown works; the harder requirement is identity proofing for the requesters who are not portal users, which includes separated members without a CAC and the third parties who make up much of the volume. Third, the portal's 40 percent is the number that explains why the desk still exists, and it will not change until the archive or a historical-data load reaches the portal; a team with a view on how VA handled historical data in its own portal migration has a comparator to offer.

Confidence: High on the documents as published. Analytical on how DHA would use them.

Standard or template What it sets Where it fits

DHA-PM 6025.02, Vol. 1 30 business days; copies free; escalation at the tenth working day The current enterprise rule and the document a revision would amend [CC2]
VA.gov records page 30 calendar days electronic, 60 paper; a delay letter; secure-message requests acknowledged in three business days The published-standard template on the shared platform [CC4]
Access for Veterans to Records Act, FY23 NDAA A plan with metrics; pending counts at 20, 90, and 365 days; reporting until 90 percent within 20 days The statutory reporting template, already written for NPRC [CC21]
HHS OCR Right of Access enforcement Request-date and completion-date logs The metric design the private sector is held to [CC18]
PHI Management Tool Disclosure accounting at the facility level The completion timestamp DHA already captures [CC22]

My read is that a team which writes its measurement proposal against the Access for Veterans to Records Act's three pending counts and the VA page's two service levels is handing DHA a metric Congress already recognizes and VA already publishes. That is the cheapest thing on the map and the one most likely to be done by a policy office without a contract at all, which is the reason to offer it as a method the office can run itself.

7. The executive order and the two bills

Confidence: High on the order's text and dates. High on the two NDAA section titles; the operative text was not reviewed for this brief and should be pulled before any proposal cites it.

Instrument What it does Date Tie to the desk
EO 14426, Sec. 2(a)(iii) Personnel file, health record, and service treatment record to VA "immediately when a service member is discharged or released" Effective within 30 days; October 8, 2026 The government path; no provision for the member's own copy [CC11]
EO 14426, Sec. 2(b) Review and modify existing IT contracts for interoperability with personnel, health, and service treatment record systems; require it in future contracts About January 6, 2027 The contract review is the moment the person's path can be written into the same instruments [CC11]
EO 14426, Sec. 2(a)(i) Updated IT systems and policy guidance for ongoing records sharing from entry into service About March 7, 2027 The systems milestone; the FEHRM joint standard aligns here [CC11]
H.R. 8800, Sec. 761 Pilot program on a secure, mobile personal health record for members of the Armed Forces In reconciliation The person's copy, by statute, if it survives conference [CC12]
S. 4784, Sec. 721 Development of a capability prototype on secure access to the health record for members of the Armed Forces In reconciliation The same requirement in the Senate's words [CC12]

Two reads. The order's 120-day contract review is a public date at which the two departments will say what their IT contracts must do, and a records desk requirement that is not in that review will wait for the next one. And the two NDAA sections are the first time Congress has put the service member's own access to the record in a defense bill in this cycle; a pilot or a prototype is a small buy with a large signal, and the office that runs it will be the office that writes the requirement for everything that follows.

8. The counterargument, stated fairly

Confidence: The strongest case against the reads above, and parts of it are right.

DOD says the 2029 digital transformation covers this. It does, by DOD's own statement to GAO, and a records desk fix that competes with the transformation program will lose. [CC1] The counter is sequencing: an early milestone inside the transformation makes the program's progress visible, and the informatics study is where DOD agreed to look.

The data is thin. GAO could not confirm reliability, 75 percent of reporting facilities saw no consistent volume increase, and the six-day and 40 percent figures are single examples. [CC1] A proposal that treats them as enterprise baselines is overreaching. The counter is that thin data is the finding, and the measurement lane is the answer.

The archive was not built for this. Probably true, and the right response is to ask, in the next EIDS exchange, whether release-of-information retrieval is in scope.

AI redaction is not ready for judgment calls. State said so. The counter is that a service member's health record is mostly identifiers, and the precedents all keep a human on every release.

Volume is flat, so the desk is fine. Volume was flat at most facilities; effort per request is what GAO measured in days. [CC1] A flat queue of six-day files is still a six-day queue.

9. What to do in the next ninety days

Confidence: Analytical.

  1. Confirm the AHLTA date. The privacy assessment set October 2026. Ask the Solution Delivery Division, or watch for the next PIA update, and read the answer against GAO's description of processors using AHLTA. [CC8][CC1]

  2. Ask the EIDS question. Can a release-of-information processor search the consolidation archive by patient, date range, and document type, and export with provenance. One question, in writing, at the next industry exchange. [CC9]

  3. Pull the EHI export status. Whether single-patient export is enabled in GENESIS decides the platform lane. The DHMSM program office and the Solution Delivery Division own the answer. [CC20]

  4. Write the measurement method. Three pending counts at 20, 90, and 365 days, a 30-calendar-day service level, and the PHI Management Tool as the completion timestamp. Offer it as a method the Privacy and Civil Liberties Office can run without a contract. [CC21][CC4][CC22]

  5. Scope a redaction pilot to identifiers. Names, SSNs, dates of birth, third-party names, with OCR for scanned records and a human approval step on every release. Cite HHS and State by document. [CC15][CC16]

  6. Read the two NDAA sections when the conference text lands. Sec. 761 and Sec. 721 are titles today; the operative text decides who runs the pilot and what it must show. [CC12]

  7. Mark January 6. The executive order's contract review is a public date, and the question to put to the two departments is whether the member's own access is in scope for the contracts they modify. [CC11]

  8. Hold the delivery lane. Secure electronic delivery reduces the improper-release count the services report; it is the smallest buy on the map with a number attached to it. [CC1]
