The lead finding for a capture team: Section 2(b) is the first executive instrument that names personnel records systems in the same sentence as health records systems and orders them made interoperable by contract. The clause carries no metric, no owner, and no reporting. The 120 days end on January 6, 2027, inside a fiscal year that started under a continuing resolution or close to it, and the modifications it produces will be bilateral, which means every incumbent gets to write the first draft of what "interoperable" means for its own system. The team that brings the definition, the measure, and the price to the contracting officer before January 6 sets the terms. The team that waits for the mod letter answers them.

Developing as this goes to press (September 13). The largest contract the Section 2(b) review reaches is the one Congress is subpoenaing executives over. VA added up to about $17 billion to the Oracle EHRM agreement on August 20 without competition. On September 2 Oracle withdrew from a House Veterans' Affairs Committee oversight hearing after accepting the invitation, and the committee voted 19 to 0 to subpoena Larry Ellison and Mike Sicilia. GAO testified that VA had not fully implemented 14 of 18 recommendations and still lacks a complete cost estimate and integrated master schedule. Military Times reported VA's total program estimate near $48 billion through 2031, which is lifecycle cost, distinct from the $27 billion contract ceiling; do not conflate them. For the January 6 review this means the incumbent with the most scope to modify has the least appetite to volunteer any, and the department with the most to gain from a real metric is the one under the sharpest oversight. [CC31][CC32]

2. What the clause says, word by word

Confidence: High. Verbatim from the order.

The operative sentence: the Secretaries "shall review and modify, to the extent consistent with law and Federal procurement policy, any existing contracts for information technology systems and software relevant to the purpose of this order, including by adding a requirement that such systems be interoperable with any other military personnel records system, health records system, or Service Treatment Records system operated by the Department of War and the Department of Veterans Affairs, and shall ensure that all future contracts for medical and personnel information technology systems and software include this requirement." [CC1]

Five things a capture team should read out of that.

"To the extent consistent with law and Federal procurement policy." That is the phrase that makes the modifications bilateral. A contracting officer cannot unilaterally add a requirement outside the scope of a competed award without inviting a scope protest, and cannot add work without an equitable adjustment where the contract's changes clause applies. The order does not attempt to override that. It defers to it. [CC1][CC9]

"Relevant to the purpose of this order." The purpose in Section 1 is personnel file and medical record sharing between the two departments. That is broad enough to reach the EHRs, the claims system, the STR repository, the identity systems, and every service personnel platform. It is also vague enough that the two Secretaries decide the list, and the list is the first deliverable to watch for. [CC1]

"Any other military personnel records system." This is the new territory. Health IT interoperability has a federal definition, a certification program, a national standard set, and a consultation partner named in the same sentence (ONC). Personnel records systems have none of that. The sentence orders the same word applied to both. [CC1][CC10]

"Operated by the Department of War and the Department of Veterans Affairs." Read literally, the interoperability target is systems operated by both departments, which excludes the Department of Labor systems the AI clause depends on and excludes Coast Guard systems under DHS unless the Secretaries read "military personnel records system" to include them. Expect a definition memo. [CC1]

"All future contracts." The forward clause is the durable one. Whatever definition the two departments adopt by January 6 becomes a standard term in every medical and personnel IT solicitation after that, which makes the definition itself the competition. [CC1]

What the sentence does not contain: a metric, an office of primary responsibility, a reporting cadence, a deadline for the modifications themselves (the 120 days is for the review), or any reference to a standard. The three deliverables in Section 6 are what a capture team can propose to fill that space.

Two structural observations.

First, on the health side the clause is close to already satisfied, and the departments will be able to say so on January 6 with a straight face: same vendor, same exchange, joint sharing sites. The exposure is at the claims interface and at the teardown, where every new GENESIS platform contract must carry the forward requirement.

Second, on the personnel side the clause is not satisfied and there is no standard to satisfy it with. That is the space where a definition brought to the government before January 6 has the most leverage.

A third observation is a finding, confirmed rather than assumed. A targeted search for a current enterprise-level prime on HAIMS, VBMS, DMDC's DEERS and RAPIDS support, VADIR, and FEHRM joint HIE support found none for any of the five. What surfaces is fragmented and mostly historical: Evolvent and Harris on legacy HAIMS, Booz Allen on legacy VBMS Core, D2 Government Solutions on one DEERS and RAPIDS task order, MITRE on a FEHRM evaluation contract. That fragmentation is why the identity seam in Section 5 has no owner. No single contract holds the reconciliation, so no single contractor is on the hook for it, and no single modification on January 6 can add it. Confirm in FPDS by keyword before capture use.

4. How a requirement enters a live contract

Confidence: High on the FAR mechanics. Medium on how OFPP will treat it, which has no public guidance yet.

FAR Part 43 governs. A modification that adds a requirement is either within the scope of the original competition, in which case the contracting officer can issue it under the contract's changes clause and the contractor is entitled to an equitable adjustment for the added work, or outside the scope, in which case it is a new procurement that must be competed or justified. The order's "consistent with law and Federal procurement policy" language acknowledges both paths and picks neither. [CC1][CC9]

For the IDIQs in scope, expect bilateral supplemental agreements at the task order level rather than base contract changes, because that is where the work and the money are. An interoperability requirement added to an Oracle EHRM task order is priced by Oracle. An interoperability requirement added to a GENESIS sustainment order is priced by Leidos, until the direct platform contracts replace it, at which point it is a term in a new sole-source award and priced there. [CC3][CC4]

Two precedents show the shape of a mandatory requirement added by clause.

CMMC. DFARS 252.204-7021 attached a mandatory cybersecurity requirement to new awards and was inserted into existing contracts by modification where the government chose to. It took years of rulemaking, and the requirement had a defined standard and an assessment regime behind it. [CC22]

Section 508. FAR Subpart 39.2 makes accessibility a required term in IT acquisitions, backed by a published technical standard. [CC23]

Both worked because the word in the clause pointed at a document. "Interoperable" in Section 2(b) points at nothing. The consultation partners named in the order are the tell for what it will point at on the health side: ONC administers the certification program, USCDI, and TEFCA, and a clause that says "conform to the current USCDI version and participate in a qualified exchange" is the path of least resistance for the two departments and for Oracle, which already does both. [CC10]

The personnel side has no ONC. DoD's data standards for person data live in the DMDC-administered identity architecture and in DoDI 1341.02, which names DEERS as the authoritative source and the services' systems as definitive, without a precedence rule between them when they disagree. A capture team that drafts that precedence rule, with a mismatch metric, and offers it as the personnel-side definition of "interoperable" is offering the government the document its own clause is missing. [CC7]

OFPP. The Administrator is named as a consultation partner, which signals a policy memo or a class deviation rather than a FAR case, given the timeline. Watch for it between now and January 6. Whatever it says about scope, equitable adjustment, and the forward clause text is the rulebook for every bid after. [CC1]

5. The seam nobody owns

Confidence: Medium. The system facts are from primary sources. The absence of a contract vehicle is my finding from this session and should be treated as provisional until a capture team pulls the DMDC and VA OIT award records.

The public issue's first break is the one with the least contract coverage. DEERS is authoritative for identity and eligibility. The service personnel systems are definitive for personnel data and feed DEERS. VADIR is VA's copy of DMDC data, matched on SSN, EDIPI, and date of birth under a computer matching agreement. VA's Master Person Index assigns the ICN and stores the crosswalk to EDIPI. [CC7][CC8]

No single office owns the reconciliation across those four. DMDC owns DEERS. The services own their systems. VA OIT owns VADIR and the MPI. When IPPS-A and DEERS disagreed in January 2023, the fix came from the Army and DMDC working the interface. When DEERS failed to send dependent changes back to IPPS-A through mid-2024, the fix came from DMDC adjusting its logic. Neither event produced a published mismatch rate, a precedence rule, or a standing owner. [CC16][CC17]

That is a data governance requirement without a contract. The nearest vehicles that could carry it:

  • A DMDC support task order, if one exists with data quality in scope. Not verified this session.
  • A DHA enterprise data task order under EIDS, which owns the MHS Information Platform and the beneficiary data feeds into it, and which has the platform to compute a DEERS-to-personnel-system mismatch count. Whether EIDS has the authority to publish one is a different question. [CC24]
  • A VA OIT identity services order covering the MPI and VADIR.
  • The DHA enterprise Commercial Solutions Opening, HT003826SC005, which any PAE or program office can use and which is built for a short white paper. [CC25]

The deliverable is small and specific: a precedence rulebook by data element, an event-driven update path in place of batch, and a monthly mismatch report by service. The evidence that the count is computable already exists in the May 10, 2024 corrected file of about 50,000 dependent updates. Someone counted it. Nobody was required to publish it. [CC17]

7. The AI clause, read as a buy

Confidence: Medium. The order text is primary. The platform inventory is from VA privacy impact assessments and the AI use case inventory, not fetched this session.

Section 2(a)(ii) orders, within 180 days, "new digital tools using artificial intelligence and other emerging capabilities" that put benefits applications and DOL job training in a single source. The operative text carries no privacy or security language beyond the statutes cited in 2(a)(i); the White House fact sheet adds "secure" and "consistent with applicable privacy protections," and the fact sheet is not the order. [CC1][CC2]

Read as a procurement, 180 days is not a new build. A new system with an authority to operate, a privacy impact assessment, and a Privacy Act system of records notice does not ship in six months from a standing start. Expect the deliverable to be an existing platform relabeled and extended: VA.gov's benefits flows, the VBA Automation Platform that already carries a FY2025 privacy impact assessment, and the VA AI use case inventory published in January 2026 are the candidates. DOL's contribution will be a data feed or a link. [CC27][CC28]

For a capture team the near-term work is not the AI. It is the integration: the single source needs the identity crosswalk from Section 5 to know that the person on VA.gov is the person in DEERS is the person in the DOL system. The AI clause depends on the seam that the identity clause never names.

The March 7, 2027 deadline for this clause coincides with the Section 2(a)(i) deadline for the permanent sharing architecture and the Section 3 TAP deadline, which means three deliverables from two departments land in the same week, subject to appropriations, with the FY2027 continuing resolution or omnibus as the constraint. [CC1]

9. The counterargument, stated fairly

Confidence: This is the strongest case the two departments can make, and parts of it are right.

The health side is done. Both departments run the same Oracle platform, the joint HIE has been live since 2020, and the joint sharing sites exist. On January 6 the departments can certify that the clinical systems are interoperable and add a forward clause that says "USCDI and a qualified exchange," which Oracle already meets. The modification costs nothing and changes nothing. That reading is available and probably the default. [CC21]

The STR process already performs. DoD can point to the 2018 follow-up audit: 86 percent timely and 92 percent complete for the Army on FY2016 claims. The public issue concedes this. The answer is that the personnel file has never been audited the same way and that the Guard and Reserve path still requires a claims processor to enter an invented period of service into VBMS to trigger a request. [CC26][CC14]

The rater gap did no harm. VA's OIG found no effect on benefits in a 30-case sample. VBA closed both recommendations. The answer is that the population was 132,770 veterans at five facilities, the sample was 30, the rollout has since restarted, and neither closed recommendation requires the rate to be computed again. [CC15]

The mods will be paper. The most likely January 6 outcome is a list of contracts, a definition memo, and a forward clause, with no metric and no money, because Section 4(b) makes everything subject to appropriations and the fiscal year started under a CR. The subpoena makes it likelier. A vendor whose chairman and chief executive have been compelled to testify about a no-bid $17 billion extension is not going to volunteer new scope into that contract in the same 120 days, and a department under that oversight is not going to open a bilateral negotiation it cannot close quietly. That is a fair prediction. It is also the reason a proposed definition with a metric, delivered before the memo is written, has leverage it would not have after. [CC31]

The order is the fourth time. NDAA FY2008 Section 1635, NDAA FY2014 Section 713, the 2013 STR agreement, and now this. The prior three produced the IPO, the FEHRM, HAIMS, and the joint HIE. They also produced the 2014 audit numbers. Mandates with a program office and a number moved; mandates with a word did not. [CC29][CC30]

10. What to do in the next ninety days

Confidence: Analytical.

  1. Pull the changes clause and scope language for every contract on the Section 3 list you hold or want. Know before January 6 whether an interoperability requirement is within scope for each, because that determines whether it is a modification with an equitable adjustment or a new competition.

  2. If you are an incumbent, write the definition first. A two-page proposed clause with a named standard on the health side (USCDI version, exchange participation) and a precedence rule with a mismatch metric on the personnel side, delivered to the contracting officer as an unsolicited technical input before the mod letter arrives. The government has 120 days and no text.

  3. If you are not an incumbent, position on the seam. The identity reconciliation deliverable in Section 5 has no owner and no vehicle. A white paper under HT003826SC005 or a task order proposal under an existing VA OIT or DMDC vehicle that offers the rulebook and the monthly report as a fixed-price service is a small bid with a large forward position. [CC25]

  4. Watch four documents. The two departments' list of contracts in scope; the OFPP memo or class deviation; the forward clause text in the first post-January solicitation for medical or personnel IT; and any DHA direct-award justification for the Oracle Health platform contract, which will be the first "future contract" to carry the requirement.

  5. Ask the October 8 question. Nothing in the order requires the departments to report whether the 30-day transfer is in place. A FOIA request or a congressional inquiry filed October 9 for the implementing memoranda is the cheapest intelligence available on how the departments read Section 2(a)(iii), and the answer tells you how they will read 2(b).

  6. Price the reconciliation as a service, not a project. The 2018 audit shows the number moved when someone owned it continuously. A recurring monthly report with a named owner is the deliverable the departments can fund inside a CR; a system build is not.
