Confidence: High on the award records from USAspending and on the GAO, OIG, and hearing documents cited. The lead finding for a capture team: the September BPA buys adoption. The technology award for External Provider Scheduling dates to September 2023, and the new agreement's own description is interoperability, implementation, training, activation, adoption, and sustainment. The six funded orders cover every network in VA's new five-VISN structure plus an enterprise order, $19.4 million in the first year against a stated $30 million value the record says may increase. Every documented failure in the pathway sits at a seam between two systems, VA has told GAO it has not yet determined how its scheduling products will interface, and the requirement for the seams is being written at CMS and in Congress before VA writes it for itself. The adoption lane is closed for the term of the BPA. The integration, data, and clock lanes are open, and whoever writes the architecture writes the next five years of orders.

Three reads. First, the only step with a dedicated buy this fiscal year is step four, and the BPA's scope words are adoption words. Second, steps five and seven are where the data does not exist, and a bidder who can show acceptance capture and record return as messages, with the acceptance date as a field in the record, is answering GAO's finding with a deliverable. Third, the row that says "None" is the market. VA's June testimony describes integrated scheduling pilots, GAO's June statement describes no architecture, and the party that writes the architecture sets the shape of every order that follows.

3. The September scheduling BPA

Confidence: High on the award record, which USAspending carries in full. Strong inference on the VISN numbering. Analytical on the reads.

Item Record
Vehicle VHA Integrated Healthcare Transformation 2.0, IDIQ 36C10X25D0017: a ten-year multiple-award IDIQ with nine SDVOSB prime contractors per the August 26, 2025 SAM.gov award notice; the $14 billion ceiling and the four functional categories (health system transformation and innovation, implementation and operations support, healthcare business enabling services, health informatics) rest on trade reporting [CC27][CC28]
BPA 36C10X26N0178, signed September 29, 2026; performance September 30, 2026 through September 29, 2031, potential end September 29, 2032; solicitation 36C10X26R0052, SDVOSB set-aside, seven offers, Strategic Acquisition Center Frederick; description: "RFO FAR 16.5 BPA established as the ordering framework for VA Enterprise Scheduling Modernization, including interoperability, implementation, training, activation, adoption, sustainment, and support across the VA ($30M value may increase over time)" [CC12]
Orders, September 30, 2026 36C10X26N0209 (VISN 1), 36C10X26N0211 (VISN 2), 36C10X26N0212 (VISN 3), 36C10X26N0213 (VISN 4), 36C10X26N0214 (VISN 5), $3,593,765.20 each, through September 29, 2027, "to support the deployment of External Provider Scheduling integrated scheduling throughout the VHA," including facility coordination and enterprise communication; plus enterprise order 36C10X26N0204 at $1,396,886.80; $19,365,712.80 obligated in all [CC12]
Awardee Agile4Vets LLC, an all-SDVOSB joint venture and one of the nine IHT 2.0 holders, per the award record and the company's own site [CC12][CC28]
Which networks The orders run VISN 1 through 5 and no further. Legacy VISN 3 was integrated into VISN 2 in October 2015, VA's VISN page now says the department "comprises 5 Veterans Integrated Service Networks," and VA's EHR deployment schedule already uses the new numbering (VISN 3 from Ohio and Indiana to the Dakotas, VISN 4 Nebraska and Missouri, VISN 5 Washington, Oregon, Idaho, and Alaska). My read is that these are RISE's five networks, which makes the first year enterprise-wide; confirm with the contracting office before stating it as fact [CC29][CC38]
Technology vendor of record WellHive, whose president testified to the House in May 2025 that the External Provider Scheduling contract was competitively awarded in September 2023; the company reports FedRAMP High authorization and the VA FAQ reports 150+ integrations [CC8][CC11]

Four reads. First, the scope language is a services scope. Interoperability, implementation, training, activation, adoption, sustainment, support: six of the seven words describe people and change management, and the seventh describes the seam. The agreement sits under a services IDIQ whose categories are implementation and operations support and health informatics, and the holder is a services joint venture. Second, the first year is already national. Five network orders of identical size plus an enterprise order, $19.4 million against a stated $30 million value, leaves about $10.6 million for years two through five unless the value rises, and the record says in its own description that it may. The shape to expect is a second-year obligation that either tops up the ceiling or converts the agreement into a larger follow-on. Third, a BPA establishes one ordering relationship, so the adoption lane under IHT 2.0 has narrowed to the holder and its team for the life of the agreement; seven offers competed for it, and the other eight IHT 2.0 holders now compete for the adjacent orders, which are the integration and data work in section 2. Fourth, the order descriptions say "External Provider Scheduling integrated scheduling," which reads as the Charleston and Atlanta pilots VA described in June, direct care and community care appointments "on the same platform," moving from pilot to deployment in every network at once. If that reading holds, the BPA is the vehicle through which direct care scheduling begins to run outside the EHR module, and the 2027 Oracle calendar, 26 hospitals across those same networks, is the constraint the deployment team will work around.

My read is that a team which writes its solution against these five documents is writing to the requirement before VA does. The SCHEDULES Act would compel a standard for the time between referral entry and appointment, with quarterly reporting and a facility ranking. [CC19] A clock that starts at consult entry and stops at the completed first visit, with veteran-requested delays recorded and reported instead of subtracted, is the NHS rules suite in VA's vocabulary, and a referral carried as 360X transactions produces the timestamps that clock needs. The integration work under the September BPA is where the first version of that gets built, whether or not anyone calls it a clock.

6. The CCN lane and the wait-time duty

Confidence: High on the OIG and VA documents and on the CCN Next Gen award records in USAspending.

VA's acting deputy assistant under secretary for integrated veteran care told the House in May 2025 that network adequacy "is not measured by individual Community Care providers, it is done through the TPAs, who then have different standards not related to MISSION Act standards." [CC8] The inspector general's August 2026 mental health review recommended adjusting the community care contracts so the contractors can deliver timely care. [CC5] VA's February 2025 plan to Congress under section 122 of the 2022 Cleland-Dole Act, which required a plan for telling veterans the expected days to care with VA and community providers and implementation by December 29, 2025, said that community availability "is reliant on extensive communication with numerous community providers" and that "there is currently no immediate solution that provides Veterans with average wait times in the community," and pointed to EPS and a community care self-scheduling pilot as the routes it would build. [CC24] Whether EPS data now serves that duty is not stated in any VA document reviewed for this brief.

The next Community Care Network contracts, CCN Next Gen, are on the record in USAspending: four IDVs signed August 31, 2026 by the Strategic Acquisition Center Fredericksburg, to Optum Public Sector Solutions (36C10G26D0024), Wellpoint Federal Solutions (0022), TriWest Healthcare Alliance (0025), and Humana Government Business (0026), with performance January 1, 2027 through December 31, 2036 and nothing obligated yet. [CC23] If the performance terms carry acceptance-date reporting, appointment-occurrence timeliness, and record return as contractor obligations, the TPA becomes the party that produces the data VA cannot. If they do not, the gap GAO measured in 2024 persists through the next contract period by design, and the integration market for steps five through seven stays open.

Two reads. A standard set by statute is a requirement with a date, and the SCHEDULES Act's quarterly report and facility ranking would create a reporting product VA does not have today. H.R. 3482 would make EPS a statutory program, which protects the adoption lane from the kind of pause VA imposed in 2024 and makes the BPA's five-year horizon more credible than the program's own history suggests.

9. The counterargument, stated fairly

Confidence: The strongest case against the reads above, and parts of it are right.

VA is building the architecture; it is in the pilots. The June testimony describes integrated scheduling pilots, the September BPA funds "integrated scheduling" deployment, and the Epic connection extends supply visibility to the largest civilian systems. [CC32][CC12][CC33] The architecture may exist inside the program and simply be unpublished. GAO's statement reports what VA officials told it, that the department has not yet determined how the products will interface, and an undocumented architecture is a risk to price into a bid, and a reason to ask for the document in the next RFI, before assuming the market is open.

The TPAs already own steps five through seven. Under CCN, the contractors manage the networks and support scheduling as needed. If CCN Next Gen carries the data obligations, the integration market shrinks to the seam between the TPA and VA. That seam is still a seam, and GAO's three percent was measured with the TPAs in place.

Capacity, again. VHA lost 783 physicians in the first eleven months of FY2026, which means more referrals outward regardless of the pathway, and a faster pathway raises the $23 billion community bill; CBO scores eligibility changes in the billions. [CC37][CC7] A bidder who promises wait-time reduction is promising what the pathway alone cannot deliver. A bidder who promises visibility is promising what the documents say is missing.

The small-business field is set. With the BPA in place, the adoption work for the life of the agreement runs through one holder. The counter is that the orders are a year long and the ceiling is a framework, and VA has shown with EPS itself that it will pause, deactivate, and restart a program inside a contract term. [CC8]

10. What to do in the next ninety days

Confidence: Analytical.

  1. Pull the BPA and the six orders from SAM.gov. The award record confirms the $30 million value, the SDVOSB set-aside, seven offers, and SAC Frederick. What it does not say is whether "VISN 1 through 5" are RISE's five networks; the evidence says yes, and the contracting office can confirm it in one call. Then read the orders' "integrated scheduling" language against the June pilots. [CC12][CC29][CC38]

  2. Map the other eight IHT 2.0 holders to the adjacent work. Integration, data, architecture, and analytics orders under the implementation and health informatics categories are the open lanes; the adoption lane is closed for the BPA's term. [CC27]

  3. Write the acceptance date as a deliverable. Any solution narrative for steps five and seven should show the referral as 360X messages with timestamps and should cite GAO-24-105308 by number. [CC3][CC15]

  4. Price to the CMS pattern. A seven-day standard and a 72-hour urgent standard for the authorization step are the numbers a congressional staffer will recognize, and the Prior Authorization API deadline in January 2027 is the technical precedent. [CC17]

  5. Read CCN Next Gen before January 1. The four IDVs are signed and on USAspending; pull the performance work statement and look for acceptance, occurrence, and record-return obligations. The lane is either in the TPA contract or it is open. [CC23]

  6. Track three bills. H.R. 3482 in the Senate, the SCHEDULES Act in both committees, and any lame-duck vehicle carrying access standards. [CC18][CC19]

  7. Build around the 2027 Oracle calendar. Four Wisconsin and Michigan sites go live in January and 22 more follow. Deployment work in networks that cross those waves will be scheduled around them, and a team with go-live capacity planning on its record has a story to tell. [CC10]

  8. Hold the records-return lane for the AI inventory. VA-25-2660 is pre-deployment, GAO's October report makes the same ask of DHA, and TEFCA is the exchange both would use. One capability, two departments. [CC35][CC36][CC16]
