The lead finding for a capture team: on September 30 the federal government's negotiating position on frontier AI expires along with the pricing, and the collateral agencies are using to plan that renewal contains at least two errors a buyer would act on. The Anthropic purchase guide carries an expiration date seven weeks early and limits the offer to Haiku and Sonnet while its own headline paragraph promises frontier models. The Gemini offer let agencies provision unlimited additional users at no cost for thirteen months and then reprices per user against whatever headcount that produced. Any agency that has not run a deprovisioning audit before September 30 is about to be priced on seats it never used, and no vendor at any published tier sells a commitment about how the model will behave after renewal. GSA has already drafted that clause. Nobody is required to honor it yet.

2. Why the leverage expires with the price

Confidence: High. Published statements and a named academic source.

The OneGov AI offers were structured as limited-time entry pricing: OpenAI at $1 per agency, Anthropic at $1 covering the executive, legislative, and judicial branches, Google at $0.47 per agency, all flowing through Carahsoft against the Multiple Award Schedule. [CC4][CC5] By May 2026 that had produced more than 120 orders, with GSA describing technology or availability extended to roughly 3.4 million across government. [CC1]

Read that figure carefully, because the distinction carries the whole dependency argument. Smeltzer's wording is availability, meaning seats an agency could have used. The only hard usage number in the public record is an OpenAI spokesperson's claim of more than a million users across federal, state, and local government, which is a party statement mixing non-federal users into the count. [CC1] Nobody has published measured federal active usage. The vendors have it. The agencies mostly do not.

Jessica Tillipman, associate dean for government procurement law studies at George Washington University, published the governing analysis in 2026 under the title "Twenty-Five Cent AI: The Hidden Costs of Promotional AI Deals." [CC14] Her scope is wider than the three expiring deals, covering promotional pricing from free to a dollar per agency across six products. Her argument is that promotional pricing looks low-risk while the real economics arrive later in lifecycle cost, operational dependency, and reduced negotiating position, and her conclusion is that the leverage agencies hold today will not survive renewal. [CC1][CC14]

Her remedy framework is worth reading before drafting anything, because it lands on the same four items a capture team would propose from the other side of the table: binding post-promotional pricing, structured pilot constraints, dependency tracking, and uniform public disclosure of lifecycle terms as a condition of OneGov participation. [CC14] That is independent corroboration from a procurement-law authority rather than a vendor talking its book.

Her illustration of behavioral lock-in is the most quotable thing in this subject, and she is describing herself. Asked what would happen if she had to abandon the account holding her own projects, she said she would weep. [CC1] A procurement law dean describing her personal switching cost in those terms is the argument making itself.

Tillipman cites the buying-in framework at FAR 3.501. [CC14] Whether these offers meet that definition is a legal question outside this analysis. The commercial dynamic is not in dispute, and the outgoing federal CIO has already said he expects vendors to price renewal against usage data collected during the promotional period. [CC1] The vendors spent a year measuring dependency. The agencies spent a year creating it.

3. Three renewal pages, three different problems

Confidence: High on the page text. The Anthropic date conflict is reported as unresolved.

Anthropic: the date does not resolve, and the scope is narrower than the announcement.

The Carahsoft OneGov purchase guide one-pager states that the offer and access expire after August 10, 2026. [CC3] That date passed three weeks ago. The main Anthropic page on the same distributor carries no expiration date at all. GSA's own Buy AI catalog lists Claude Enterprise at $1 through September 2026 for all federal, and FedScoop reports a September 30 expiration. [CC1][CC2]

Treat GSA's catalog as the operative date and treat the discrepancy as a finding rather than noise. A capture team or an agency pricing a renewal off the reseller one-pager is working from a date seven weeks early.

The same one-pager carries the more consequential problem. Its fine print limits the offer to "Access to Haiku and Sonnet base models only," priced per organization, with eligibility to federal civilian, judicial, and legislative agencies. Its headline paragraph describes the company's frontier models with continuous updates as new capabilities release. [CC3] Those two statements sit in the same document.

That contradiction is the public issue's thesis appearing inside federal procurement collateral. An agency that built a workflow expecting frontier-class reasoning, on the strength of the headline, was provisioned the base tier. Nobody had to change a model for that gap to exist. It was there at signature, in writing, two paragraphs apart.

Google: the free-provisioning trap.

The Carahsoft Gemini page confirms $0.47 per agency through September 30 and states that at the end of the promotional period, "customers must renew at a per user price." The same page describes a standard environment including a thousand users, with additional users provisioned at no cost for the duration of the promotional period. [CC7]

The incentive during the promotional window runs exactly opposite to the incentive at renewal. Agencies were encouraged to provision freely for thirteen months, and renewal converts to per-user pricing against whatever headcount that produced. An agency that never tracked provisioned seats against active seats is about to be priced on the former.

OpenAI: the fixed end date.

The Carahsoft page states the term runs 8/14/2025 to 9/30/2026 with the end date fixed no matter the customer's start date, with pricing tiered by user count from one thousand to fifty thousand, customers not trued up, purchase on GSA Schedule, promo SKU CHATGPTENTBASE-FED. [CC6]

The trade reporting that subscriptions do not automatically renew does not appear on that page in either direction. [CC1] The fixed end date is the stronger and verifiable fact, and it has a consequence nobody has priced. An agency that onboarded in June 2026 bought roughly four months of a fourteen-month promotional window at the same nominal price, and arrives at renewal with a fraction of the dependency data the vendor will use to price it.

4. The clause that already exists

Confidence: High on the clause text. Medium on finalization.

The public issue establishes that no vendor sells a behavior guarantee at any published tier. The relevant capture fact is that the government has already drafted the requirement, and the drafting is better than either the market or the commentary has noticed.

Proposed GSAR 552.239-7001, titled "Basic Safeguarding of Data Within Large Language Model Artificial Intelligence Systems (LLMs)," published at 91 FR 36559 on June 17, 2026 with comments closed August 3, provides at paragraph (j)(2): [CC11]

(i) The Government reserves the right to conduct automated assessments of the LLM, as deployed and configured for government users, at any time using its own benchmarks. These evaluations may assess bias, truthfulness, safety, unsolicited ideological content, and other factors determined by the Government in order to facilitate evaluations.

(ii) The Contractor must provide tools and interfaces that enable the Government to run its benchmarks in an automated fashion to test the production LLM and identify any material gaps.

(iv) All benchmarks, test data, and methodologies developed or used by the Government for such assessments are considered Government Data. The Government is under no obligation to disclose or provide access to the underlying data, methodologies, or systems.

Three details drive the capture position.

The clause reaches the LLM as deployed and configured for government users. That is the configuration layer above the weights, which is precisely where the documented degradation happens and precisely what a FedRAMP authorization does not describe.

Subparagraph (ii) is an affirmative build obligation on the contractor rather than a permission granted to the Government. Somebody has to engineer the test interface, which makes it a scoped, priceable deliverable with engineering lead time longer than any proposal cycle.

Subparagraph (iv) keeps the Government's benchmarks and methodology confidential. That is what makes the test un-gameable, and it is the commercial argument for the assurance services play: a benchmark the vendor can see is a benchmark the vendor can optimize against.

The direction of travel supports betting on this clause. The June 17 text is a revised, narrowed draft issued as Notice-MVAC-2026-01 after industry pushback on a broader earlier version. The scope narrowed from AI systems generally to LLMs while the compliance demands broadened. [CC15] A rule that gets more demanding as it gets narrower has institutional weight behind it.

5. What the drift measurement actually supports

Confidence: High. Read from the paper and its published abstract.

DriftBench, sole-authored by Gianluigi Vitale of Universitas Mercatorum and presented at MLSys 2026, holds model weights constant, varies only serving infrastructure, and measures 236,985 prompt-response pairs across 105 configurations spanning five models, four GPU platforms, three serving frameworks, and three numeric precisions. Drift varies up to 186-fold by workload: math flips at 16.74 percent, code generation at 0.09 percent. [CC13]

Use that spread precisely rather than loosely. A validation run on code generation would surface roughly half a percent of the flip rate the same change produces on math. That comparison is arithmetic on the paper's two published figures rather than a finding the paper states, and it should be presented that way in any technical volume.

The headline number needs the same discipline. The blocked upgrade flipped 23.85 percent of safety prompts, and those flips ran in both directions, safe to unsafe and unsafe to safe, with the net unsafe rate barely moving. The classification was performed by LlamaGuard-3-8B at 85.21 percent recall. The paper's claim is that operators cannot predict which prompts will be affected. [CC13]

For acceptance testing, unpredictability is the worse finding and the better argument. A measurable decline can be corrected for in a threshold. Instability with no predictable locus cannot, which is exactly why the remedy is a workload-specific canary set rather than a benchmark score.

One further finding is directly useful to a program office. The paper separates predictable from unpredictable drift: hardware and precision changes exhibit systematic drift that can be modeled in advance, while framework and model changes are idiosyncratic and require re-measurement each time. [CC13] That distinction tells an agency which infrastructure events can be handled by prediction and which demand full revalidation, and it is a defensible basis for a tiered testing regime in a technical approach.

The mechanism in the blocked upgrade is worth naming because of how ordinary it is: H100 at FP16 to B200 at FP8, same serving framework. A hardware refresh plus a precision change.

6. What agencies do not hold on October 1

Confidence: High on the absence. This is the thirty-day argument in one section.

Nothing in the three reseller pages addresses data portability at expiration. [CC3][CC6][CC7] Google's renewal converts to per-user, OpenAI's window closes on a fixed date with tiered pricing, Anthropic has published no renewal terms at all, and GSA has confirmed only that some offers get extended, some get replaced, and prices eventually increase. [CC2]

Set that against what the proposed clause would guarantee: open-format export with documented APIs, migration tooling, certified deletion, and Government data ownership. [CC11] None of it is in force.

So on October 1 an agency holds a year of accumulated work inside a system, no published export obligation, no version-pinning right, no notice obligation covering model or configuration changes, and an authorization written at a layer that does not describe behavior. [CC10] The switching cost Tillipman describes is only partly technical. Most of it is the absence of any contractual instrument that would make switching possible.

That gap is the entire pitch. It is also why the assurance and portability work is a services opportunity before it is a product opportunity, because somebody has to build export tooling, canary sets, scheduled regression runs, and an evidence package an authorizing official can accept. No incumbent is scoped for it, because no contract requires it yet.

7. The four artifacts

Confidence: High. OMB memorandum text.

OMB M-26-04, issued December 11, 2025 under EO 14319, applies to any LLM procured by an agency, requires compliance terms in solicitations and orders issued after that date, and specifies four minimum vendor disclosures. [CC12]

Every OneGov renewal executed after September 30 is a new order, which has to carry M-26-04 compliance language on its own footing rather than inheriting 2025 terms, and 552.239-7001 obligations if the rule finalizes. A vendor arriving with the four artifacts already produced, plus a documented regression testing method, is answering questions the incumbent has not been asked.

Acceptable Use Policy. Model, system, and data cards. End user resources. An end user feedback mechanism. [CC12] Cheap to produce now, expensive under deadline.

8. The counterargument

Confidence: Medium. The strongest case against acting now.

GSA may extend. Smeltzer's August 13 statement points that way, and an extension at promotional pricing would push this analysis to a later date. [CC2] A team that spends September rewriting technical volumes around behavior terms may find the market unchanged in October.

The clause is proposed, not final. Comments closed August 3, no final rule has published, and law firm analyses treat it as still in flux. [CC11][CC15] Building a capability around a proposed rule is a bet on a regulatory outcome, and proposed rules die.

And the operational harm is undocumented. The DriftBench upgrade was blocked, meaning the process worked, the net unsafe rate barely moved, and the classifier itself runs at 85.21 percent recall. [CC13] No federal AI incident attributable to silent behavior change appears in the public record. A contracting officer can reasonably call this a hypothetical risk with a hypothetical remedy.

The counter to the counter is the calendar and the collateral. Renewal happens regardless of what the FAR council does, terms secured in a renewal persist regardless of the rule, and the two contradictions documented in Section 3 are live errors in the material agencies are using to plan right now. Those do not require a regulatory outcome to be worth a phone call.

9. What to do in the next thirty days

Run the deprovisioning audit, and run it first. Any customer on the Gemini offer provisioned users at zero marginal cost for thirteen months and gets repriced per user at renewal. [CC7] The gap between provisioned and active seats is pure renewal cost, it is calculable this week, and nobody has an incentive to raise it except you. This is the most immediate item in the window.

Flag the Anthropic date and scope conflict to any customer on that offer. The reseller one-pager says access expired August 10; GSA's catalog says September 2026. [CC1][CC3] The same one-pager promises frontier models and delivers Haiku and Sonnet base. An agency that built a workflow on the headline should know before it prices a renewal, and being the one who tells them is worth more than a capability brief.

Call every customer with a OneGov order and ask one question. Has anyone priced October 1, and does the renewal instrument contain any notice obligation covering model or configuration changes. Most will not have an answer, and the absence is the opening.

Draft the three clauses into your next technical volume. Notice before model, configuration, or serving-infrastructure changes rather than retirements alone. A version-pinning right with a defined support period. Regression testing against the government's workload rather than published benchmarks. No competitor located offers these, and offering them costs nothing until accepted.

Price the (j)(2)(ii) build obligation now. Automated government benchmarking against your production model requires an interface, a harness, and a support commitment, and subparagraph (iv) means you will never see the tests. [CC11] Engineering lead time exceeds proposal lead time, which is the argument for scoping it before a final rule rather than after.

Produce the four M-26-04 artifacts this month. They apply to any LLM sold to any agency, and every post-September renewal is a new order. [CC12]

Scope the assurance and portability service separately from the product. Canary set construction from agency traffic, scheduled regression runs keyed to the predictable-versus-idiosyncratic distinction, export tooling, and an evidence package for an authorizing official. [CC13] Sellable as a modification into an existing vehicle rather than waiting for a competition.

Set an alert on the successor instrument. Whatever replaces the OneGov AI offers is the largest federal AI acquisition event of the fiscal year, and there is no public plan for it. [CC1][CC2]
