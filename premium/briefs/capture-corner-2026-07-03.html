This issue maps the second layer the May memo and the draft FY2027 NDAA are building on top of it: the governance mandate, the department-wide measurement build, and the service human-performance strategies now landing on a statutory clock. This layer is newer, softer, and mostly still forecast. Confidence is lower here than in the recompete map, and it is marked as such throughout.

2. What the memo is actually standing up

Confidence: Moderate (memo reporting; no solicitation on record).

The recompetes fund people: coaches, trainers, specialists embedded at the brigade. The memo funds something different. It orders the Department to build a way to measure what those people produce, across every component, on one dashboard, held to weapon-system rigor.

Read structurally, that is three distinct demands. A common data standard, so five services stop reporting five different things. A place to hold the data, the department-wide dashboard the memo names. And a governance authority with the reach to compel both, which is why the effort sits under the Assistant Secretary for Readiness and not inside any single service or the health system.

The Army is the tell for where this goes. It has already moved past the argument, procuring its own human-performance management system that pulls biometrics, assessments, and self-reported data into role-based views for its teams. One service solved its own version. The memo's harder problem is comparability across all of them, which is a governance and standards job before it is a technology buy.

For a capture lead, the near-term signal is not a NAICS or a ceiling. It is the Tata action plan due this week, because that document is the first public statement of how the Department intends to instrument this and on what timeline.

3. The statutory turn

Confidence: Moderate to low (draft legislation; confirm at enactment).

A memo is an executive-branch preference. It can be reversed by the next signature. What makes this cycle different is that Congress is moving to convert it into law.

The draft FY2027 NDAA reported out of the Senate Armed Services Committee carries two provisions worth a capture lead's attention. Sec. 766 establishes Warfighter Performance Optimization and Total Force Fitness as a named initiative in statute. Sec. 1079 requires each military department to produce a service-wide human-performance strategy within 180 days, and to identify in it the requirements, policies, technologies, and services it intends to acquire.

Section 1079 is the one that turns a wellness posture into a procurement pipeline. If it survives conference in anything close to its current form, each service has a statutory deadline to put on paper what it plans to buy. That is a forecast document generator, on a clock, for four services at once. The section numbers and 180-day trigger are draft figures and must be confirmed against the enacted text.

Confidence: Moderate on sequence, low on dates.

Anchor point. The action plan due this week and the September strategic plan are the two documents that convert this from posture to plan. If either slips or lands vague, the whole layer stays forecast into FY2027.

Disruption risks. A continuing resolution, a change of leadership at Readiness or P&R, or the loss of Sec. 1079 in conference each pushes this layer right or dissolves it back into service-by-service discretion.

5. How this layer differs from the recompetes

Confidence: Analytical.

The recompetes in the July 1 brief run on human-performance staffing lines, 541612 for the Army coaches, 621340 for the SOCOM clinical model. The measurement layer, when it surfaces as a solicitation, is unlikely to ride those. Instrumentation, data standardization, and a department-wide dashboard read as an IT and data-services requirement, a different size standard and a different competitive field than the staffing recompetes.

That divergence is the single most useful thing to carry out of this brief. The company positioned to field embedded coaches is not automatically the company positioned to build the measurement backbone, and the Department has signaled it wants both. Which office owns the data buy, Readiness, P&R, or a service program office, is not yet established on the public record, and that ownership question is the one to track before any requirement is written.

6. Bottom line

The staffing market is mature and mapped. This layer is neither. It is a governance mandate with a measurement build attached, backed for the first time by a peer-reviewed return figure and, if the NDAA holds, by statute. The near-term intelligence is documentary, not contractual: the action plan this week, the strategic plan in September, the NDAA in conference, and the service strategies that follow. Watch the documents. The solicitations are a cycle behind them.
