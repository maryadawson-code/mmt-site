Confidence: High on every budget figure, read from the FY2027 MHS justification (Volume 1, PBA-19, OP-5, and PB-31R exhibits). High on the CR terms and legislative status. Medium on the timing calls in Sections 3 and 4, which are analytical reads of how standard CR language and option cycles interact. Analytical on the scenarios in Section 4.

The lead finding for a capture team: fiscal 2027 moves money from hospital support labor to hospital capability. The contract dollars come out of In-House Care, the same budget activity that runs the military hospitals, and the growth goes to digital health, partnership infrastructure at priority hospitals, data, blood, and trauma systems. The stopgap freezes both moves at last year's rate, so the cut and the growth arrive together when a full-year bill passes. A support contractor whose option date falls after that bill, and a technology firm whose growth pursuit depends on it, are watching the same vote.

3. The insourcing exposure, function by function

Confidence: High on the budget language. Analytical on which contract types it reaches.

The justification names three function families for reduced reliance on contracted labor inside MTFs: administrative, clinical support, and operational. [CC3] It does not define them. Read against how MTF support is typically bought, my read of where the language lands first:

Function family Typical contracted work in MTFs Exposure read
Administrative Front desk and appointing, medical records and release of information, coding and billing support, credentialing, administrative support to clinical departments High. Lowest barrier to civil service replacement; already the target of the FY2026 contractor FTE reduction
Clinical support Medical assistants, LPNs, technicians, sterile processing, pharmacy technicians, scribes Medium. Hiring lag and pay competition limit how fast civil servants replace contractors; ambient listening growth substitutes for part of the scribe workforce
Operational Facility operations support, patient movement, logistics support inside the hospital, readiness and training administration Medium. Some of this work likely migrates into the +$51.0M readiness and logistics offices at priority MTFs

Three facts sharpen the read. In-House Care already shed 1,260 contractor FTEs between FY2025 and FY2026, and the justification attributes that to planned increases in military and civilian personnel inside the hospitals. [CC3] Average uniformed strength in In-House Care rises by 1,486 in FY2027 while In-House Care civilian FTEs are flat, which means the uniformed side carries the growth. [CC3] And the contract services cut is $294.3 million in dollars against a flat contractor FTE count for FY2027, which reads as consolidation and repricing of the remaining work more than a headcount purge. [CC3]

Timing. A continuing resolution funds last year's rate, and last year's rate includes last year's contracts. My read is that the reduction executes after a full-year appropriation, which puts option exercises, bridge decisions, and recompetes for MTF administrative and clinical support services between roughly February and September 2027 at the most risk of descoping. A contract whose option falls inside the CR window is likelier to be exercised as is.

Where the work goes. Rescoping of MTFs with underutilized capabilities is in the same paragraph as the priority-MTF investment. [CC3] Support contracts at facilities outside the priority set carry two risks at once: the insourcing language and the rescoping language. Support contracts at Core 15 facilities carry the insourcing risk and a demand offset, because recapture adds patients.

Full-year appropriation with the split, no transfer authority Two accounts enacted, no cross-account authority beyond general transfer limits Every recaptured case costs COMP while its network savings stay in PSCP [CC13] Core 15 hospitals hit COMP cost pressure by summer; expect reprogramming requests and caution on recapture targets; support contract cuts may deepen to offset
C. Full-year continuing resolution Government funded at FY2026 structure for the year The new 0146D account lacks a prior-year appropriation to continue; reported analysis says a full-year CR would leave it unfunded as a separate account, so the split slides to FY2028 [CC14] Growth increases never arrive; the In-House Care contract cut never arrives either; status quo with FY2026 money and FY2027 prices

Scenario B is the one to plan for, because it is the default if conference does nothing special. The bill summaries and the administration's statement on H.R. 8800 say nothing about transfer authority between the two accounts. [CC7][CC8] Scenario C is the one that looks like relief for support contractors and is a loss for everyone selling growth.

The calendar makes C more plausible than usual. The midterms fall November 3, the CR expires December 11 inside the lame-duck session, and the Senate has not taken up its authorization bill since July. [CC1][CC9] Watch the first week of December.

5. Recapture as a market

Confidence: High on the policy statements. Analytical on the demand map.

Recapture cuts purchased care spending only if military hospitals can find, schedule, treat, and document the returning patients. Every step in that chain is a buy.

Referral routing. DHA said in June that referrals to its new Specialty Care Centers of Excellence will be managed by central referral management centers, with specialty-specific eligibility criteria going to providers, referral managers, and the managed care support contractors. [CC15] The T-5 contractors route the referrals; Humana Government Business holds the East Region and received a CY2026 option of about $7.34 billion, with the CY2027 option decision reported for December. [CC16] Referral management workflow, criteria logic, and leakage reporting sit between DHA and the contractors who control the referral stream today.

Specialty capacity. The TRICARE Prime specialty access standard is four weeks. [CC17] Via has committed to reducing the time service members spend nondeployable, and the justification reports active duty access below the benchmark since FY2023. [CC5][CC3] My read is that active duty specialty access tightens in FY2027 while the Core 15 pursue more complex cases, which makes specialty scheduling, capacity modeling, and template management the first operational choke point. The Inspector General found DHA's access dashboards unreliable enough that staff built workarounds, so measurement work comes bundled with it. [CC18]

Patient experience. Military Prime enrollees scored below the CAHPS benchmark in FY2025 while civilian Prime and Select met it. [CC3] The $200.4 million digital health line, video visits in particular, is the budget's answer to getting beneficiaries back through the door. [CC3]

Partnership data. GAO found DHA cannot count its civilian partnerships and that 39 percent of personnel with partnership clinical activity on readiness dashboards logged no partnership hours in the timecard system; DHA concurred with guidance to collect complete clinical activity data and partially concurred on the inventory. [CC19] Inventory, timekeeping integration, and readiness credit for partnership hours are an unfunded requirement with a GAO recommendation behind it.

Pharmacy. The justification calls the MTF pharmacy the lowest-cost option and expects medication delivery inside MTFs to rise with care. [CC3] Direct care pharmacy operations, automation, and supply move with recapture.

6. The retiree math

Confidence: High on the payer rules and the fund. Analytical on the implication.

The justification names retirees among the beneficiaries military hospitals must reattract, and DHA's June Centers of Excellence announcement promised more specialty choice inside the system to beneficiaries past, present, and future. [CC3][CC15] Older beneficiaries carry the complexity surgical currency needs.

Their care runs on different money. The Medicare-Eligible Retiree Health Care Fund pays the Department's share of care for Medicare-eligible retirees in both direct care and private sector care, and the new Private Sector Care Program excludes TRICARE For Life. [CC3][CC20] In the network, Medicare pays first and TRICARE For Life second; in a military hospital, those beneficiaries are seen on a space-available basis and the Department carries the cost. [CC21]

For a capture team that means two things. Retiree recapture creates direct care workload without creating PSCP savings, so any business case that sells retiree volume as a cost reduction will fail review; sell it as currency and readiness. And the hospitals taking that volume need the specialty capacity, scheduling, and pharmacy support in Section 5 more than anyone, because space-available care is exactly the care that loses its slot when active duty demand rises.

9. The counterargument, stated fairly

Confidence: This is the strongest case against the reads above, and parts of it are right.

Insourcing announcements rarely survive contact with hiring. Civilian hiring lags, pay competition in clinical support is brutal, and the justification itself describes FY2026 civilian growth as overcoming hiring lags. [CC3] A contract cut the hospitals cannot backfill turns into a bridge. That is a fair prediction for clinical support, and the reason Section 3 rates it medium. It is weaker for administrative work, which is where the FY2026 contractor reduction already landed.

The budget is a request. Congress can restore contract services, reject the split, or pass a full-year CR, and appropriators historically protect hospital operations. All true. The direction still matters, because DHA writes its option decisions and acquisition strategies to the request it submitted, and the incumbents who wait for the final number lose the months when requirements get written.

Recapture has been promised before. The reforms that followed the December 2016 legislation moved hospital management to DHA and pushed care into the network, and the system's capacity problems are documented by GAO. [CC29] The answer is that this budget names the mechanism, puts uniformed staff growth behind it, and ties it to a readiness rationale with a Director's personal commitment. That does not guarantee execution. It does guarantee that the requirements, the white papers, and the evaluation criteria for FY2027 buys will be written in recapture language.

10. What to do in the next ninety days

Confidence: Analytical.

  1. Map every MTF support contract you hold to the three function families in Section 3. For each, list the option or end date, the facility, and whether the facility is a likely priority hospital. Contracts with administrative scope at facilities off the priority list and option dates after a full-year bill are the top of the risk list.

  2. Build the insourcing transition offer before the government asks. A priced, fixed-fee transition and knowledge transfer service for functions moving to civil service positions turns a cut into a smaller, defensible task order. The government needs someone to train the replacements.

  3. Reposition growth capture onto the five lines that rise. Digital health, the Improve Wartime Readiness line at priority hospitals, trauma and registry data, blood, and pharmaceutical supply. Write white papers to the enterprise CSO now so they are in the queue when the money arrives. [CC22]

  4. Write the recapture business case in readiness terms. Lead with currency and access for active duty, show the purchased care effect only for Prime enrollees, and never claim savings on retiree volume. Section 6 explains why a reviewer will catch it.

  5. Ask for the transfer authority answer. If you have a relationship with DHA J-8 or with appropriations staff, the question of cross-account authority is the single most useful piece of intelligence for the year. Scenario B changes the size of every Core 15 requirement.

  6. Prepare for the January 6 language. Every medical or personnel IT proposal after that date should carry an interoperability paragraph traceable to EO 14426, with a named standard and a measure. [CC10]

  7. Track the open season and CAHPS signals. Prime enrollment shifts after December 8 and the next CAHPS release are the earliest public evidence of whether recapture is working, and they move requirement priorities by spring.
