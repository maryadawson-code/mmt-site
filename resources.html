Getting Started in Federal Health IT

New to government contracting or defense health? This is your on-ramp. No acronym soup, no consultant-speak.

1. Get Registered

Before any federal agency can give you money, you need a Unique Entity Identifier (UEI) and an active registration in SAM.gov. You'll also get a CAGE code during registration. Allow 10+ business days. It must be renewed annually or you're locked out of awards. Registration is free. If someone charges you, it's a scam.

2. Know Your NAICS Codes

NAICS codes classify what you do. The government uses them to determine size standards and set-aside eligibility. Common health IT codes: 541512 (Computer Systems Design), 541511 (Custom Programming), 518210 (Cloud/Data Processing).

3. Understand the Acquisition Lifecycle

The basic flow: Market Research → Solicitation (RFI, then RFP/RFQ) → Proposal Submission → Evaluation → Award → Performance → Closeout. The whole process is governed by the FAR. Changes during performance always flow through the Contracting Officer.

4. Find Opportunities

Active solicitations post on SAM.gov. Agency forecast sites publish upcoming procurements before they hit SAM. Track contract vehicles like IDIQs, GWACs, and BPAs. Read the RFIs too. They tell you what the government is thinking about buying.

5. Consider Entry Paths

A GSA Schedule gives you a pre-negotiated contract that agencies can buy from directly. The SBA's 8(a) program offers mentorship and set-aside access. SBIR/STTR programs fund R&D. Subcontracting under an established prime is another way in. There's no single right answer, but there is a wrong one: trying to win a $1B IDIQ as your first contract.

SAM.gov Registration Checklist

What You'll Need

  • ‣ EIN (Employer Identification Number) or TIN
  • ‣ UEI (assigned during SAM registration)
  • ‣ Bank account info for electronic funds transfer
  • ‣ NAICS codes for your primary business activities
  • ‣ Company size and ownership details

Key Facts

  • ‣ Registration is free. Period.
  • ‣ Allow 10+ business days for processing
  • ‣ Must renew annually or registration goes inactive
  • ‣ CAGE code assigned automatically
  • ‣ Required before any contract, grant, or cooperative agreement

Pillar 1

MHS Technology

The Military Health System serves 9.6M beneficiaries across 700+ facilities worldwide. These are the systems, programs, and organizations that run it.

Key Terms

MHS GENESIS

DoD's EHR built on Oracle Health. The single largest health IT deployment in federal history.

DHA

Defense Health Agency. Runs military healthcare operations, TRICARE, pharmacy, and health IT.

JOMIS

Joint Operational Medicine Information Systems. Medical IT for deployed and combat environments.

Role 1 / 2 / 3

Medical treatment echelons with different care capability and IT requirements at each level.

Also: MHS, MTF, TRICARE, DHP, EHR, OPMED, NDAA, ASD(HA)

Key Leadership

ASD(HA) — Keith Bass

Sets policy and budget priorities for the entire Military Health System.

DHA Director

Operational authority over MTFs, TRICARE, pharmacy, and health IT systems.

OPMED

Formerly PEO DHMS. DoD's lead for medical development and acquisition.

DHA Enterprise IT

Health IT infrastructure, cybersecurity, and data strategy across the MHS.

Pillar 2

VA Health IT

The VA operates the largest integrated healthcare system in the U.S. Its technology modernization is one of the most watched programs in federal IT.

Key Terms

VA EHR (Oracle Health)

The VA's EHR modernization program. Same platform as MHS GENESIS, different deployment challenges.

VistA

VA's legacy EHR, built in-house starting in the 1970s. Being replaced, but still running at most sites.

JLV

Joint Longitudinal Viewer. Bridges DoD and VA patient records for continuity of care.

VHA

Veterans Health Administration. 1,300+ facilities, 9M+ enrolled veterans.

Also: T4NG, Connected Care

Key Leadership

Under Secretary for Health (VHA)

Leads the largest integrated healthcare system in the U.S.

VA CIO / OIT

Manages VA's $4B IT budget, EHR modernization, and enterprise integration.

Office of Connected Care

Telehealth, remote monitoring, VA Video Connect, My HealtheVet.

VA OIG

Inspector General. Audits and investigations, especially on EHR deployment.

Key Resources

VA OIT — digital.va.gov↗ VA EHR Modernization — digital.va.gov↗ VA Lighthouse APIs — developer.va.gov↗ Connected Care — connectedcare.va.gov↗ VA Open Data — data.va.gov↗

Pillar 3

Contracting & Acquisition

How the federal government buys health IT. The rules, vehicles, roles, and mechanics that shape every deal.

Key Terms

FAR

Federal Acquisition Regulation. The primary rule book for government procurement.

IDIQ

Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity. The bread and butter of federal IT procurement.

GWAC

Government-Wide Acquisition Contracts. CIO-SP4, SEWP, Alliant 3. The big doors.

CO / KO

The only person who can legally bind the government to a contract.

Also: BPA, Task Order, RFP, RFI, SOW, PWS, pWin, COR, OTA, Fixed-Price vs Cost-Plus, Small Business Set-Asides, SAM.gov, GovCon

Congressional Oversight

HASC

House Armed Services Committee. Authorizes defense spending. Writes the NDAA.

SASC

Senate Armed Services Committee. Their NDAA markup often diverges from the House version.

HVAC

House Veterans' Affairs Committee. Oversees VA healthcare and EHR modernization.

SVAC

Senate Veterans' Affairs Committee. VA leadership confirmation hearings happen here.

Pillar 4

Compliance & Cybersecurity

The security frameworks, certifications, and compliance requirements that gate market access. If you can't pass these, you can't sell to the government.

Key Terms

ATO

Authority to Operate. The security approval needed before going live on a government network.

FedRAMP

Security certification required for any cloud product serving federal agencies.

CMMC

Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification. Becoming table stakes for DoD contractors.

Zero Trust

DoD's mandated security architecture. Never trust, always verify.

Also: NIST 800-171, NIST 800-53, STIGs, COTS

Key Resources

Pillar 5

Interoperability

The standards, frameworks, and networks that connect health systems across DoD, VA, and the broader healthcare ecosystem.

Key Terms

FHIR

Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources. The modern data standard mandated by the 21st Century Cures Act.

HL7

The foundational family of healthcare data exchange standards. FHIR is its modern evolution.

TEFCA

Trusted Exchange Framework and Common Agreement. The national infrastructure for health data exchange.

USCDI

The standardized set of health data classes that systems must be able to exchange. Updated annually.

Also: FEHRM, Joint HIE, eHealth Exchange, Carequality, DirectTrust
