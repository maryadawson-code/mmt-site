# The Formulary Storm

## Five P&T Committee cycles. Zero Beneficiary Advisory Panel reviews. The DoD Pharmacy & Therapeutics Committee meets again May 6-7. Nobody outside DHA pharmacy operations is talking about what that means.

---

Friends,

A retiree in Tampa picks up his Tier 3 prescription. The price has changed since January. The criteria for refill may shift again in the next sixty days. His prescriber does not know that yet. The pharmacist does not know that yet. The benefit administrator running the formulary file does not know which of approximately twelve months of queued formulary decisions will release first, in what order, or against which clinical pathway.

The retiree pays his copay and walks out.

The DoD Pharmacy & Therapeutics Committee meets May 6 and 7, 2026. This is the fifth quarterly cycle to convene since the Department of Defense paused all advisory committee operations on March 7, 2025, and the fifth in a row whose recommendations cannot pass through Beneficiary Advisory Panel review. The BAP has not met since December 18, 2024. All UFBAP member appointments concluded April 25, 2025. No successor chair has been named.

Under 10 U.S.C. § 1074g(c), the Secretary of Defense must consider the BAP's comments before implementing the uniform formulary or implementing changes to it. The legal architecture conditions formulary action on a procedural step that the Department's own pause has prevented from occurring.

About twelve months of tier shifts, new drug entries, step therapy criteria, and coverage decisions are about to release into the TRICARE pharmacy benefit in compressed sequence once a reconstituted BAP convenes and works the queue. The current charter expires August 1, 2026.

This issue walks the freeze, the legal architecture, the math, and the downstream implications for the senior federal health audience MMT serves. The conclusion is plain: the storm is a procedural artifact of an advisory committee pause that swept up a statutorily-required body. The pharmacy benefit absorbs the consequence.

---

## The freeze

On March 7, 2025, Joe Kasper, then Chief of Staff to Secretary of Defense Hegseth, signed a memorandum titled "Review of Department of Defense Advisory Committees" pursuant to delegated authority from the Secretary. The memorandum directed a 45-day pause and review of all DoD advisory committees. It took effect immediately.

The administration's review opened with a February 13, 2025 email from the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Personnel and Readiness asking every active committee to justify its existence by February 21. The March 7 memorandum formalized the pause. On April 25, 2025, members of all DoD advisory committees were dismissed pending the outcome of the review. In May, the review recommended terminating 14 advisory groups. The Defense Advisory Committee on Women in the Services was formally disestablished effective September 17, 2025, with the Federal Register notice published November 18.

The Uniform Formulary Beneficiary Advisory Panel was not on the termination list. It survived the review on paper. But it was on the pause list. And the pause meant the Panel could not legally convene.

The distinction matters. DACOWITS is a policy advisory body the Department can choose to terminate as a matter of executive judgment. The UF BAP is statutorily required. Title 10 of the United States Code, Section 1074g(c), directs the Secretary of Defense to establish the Panel "to review and comment on the development of the uniform formulary." The same subsection requires the Secretary to consider those comments before implementing the uniform formulary or implementing changes to it. No BAP comment means no Secretary action. No Secretary action means the proposed formulary change does not take effect.

The pause did not formally violate the statute. It paused the architecture that lets the statute function. The result was the same.

---

## The math

The DoD Pharmacy and Therapeutics Committee meets approximately quarterly and continued to meet through the freeze. P&T is a closed government body under 10 U.S.C. § 1074g(b) that does not require Federal Advisory Committee Act notice. Its work product is recommendations to the Director, Defense Health Agency. Those recommendations cannot become operative until the BAP, a FACA-governed body that does require notice and member appointments, has commented.

From the December 18, 2024 BAP meeting through this week, five consecutive P&T Committee cycles have produced recommendations no BAP has reviewed. May 2025. August 2025. November 2025. February 2026. The May 6-7, 2026 cycle convening this week. Each cycle reviewed therapeutic drug classes, evaluated newly approved drugs, and updated utilization management criteria. Each generated a set of recommendations the BAP must comment on before any action becomes final.

Recommendations queued. They did not stop. They did not get withdrawn. They did not become inoperative. They simply waited for the procedural body the statute requires.

The P&T cycles cover predictable categories of pharmaceutical economy. Therapeutic class reviews on the May 1-2, 2024 cycle covered basal insulin analogs. The February 2024 cycle covered growth-stimulating hormones. The May 3-4, 2023 cycle covered antilipidemics including PCSK9 inhibitors. The February 2022 cycle conducted a multi-class review of oncological agents covering PARP inhibitors, BTK inhibitors, mTOR inhibitors, multiple TKIs, and BCL-2 inhibitors in a single combined session. Each class review produced tier placements, prior authorization criteria, step therapy requirements, and effective dates. The pattern is established. The queued backlog from five cycles touches at minimum five or six therapeutic classes that have not had a federal formulary update in over a year, plus newly approved drugs across the same period that have been waiting for their first formulary placement decision.

Three concrete examples of pharmaceutical activity during the freeze period the federal formulary did not respond to in normal cycle. First, GLP-1 receptor agonists. The class has expanded clinically across that twelve-month window with new indications, new formulations, and a federal pricing posture that includes the TrumpRx integration agreed by Express Scripts in February 2026. Second, basal insulin biosimilars. Multiple new biosimilar entries have hit the market since the August 2017 last federal formulary review for the class, with several more entering during the freeze. Third, the broader category of newly approved drugs whose first federal formulary placement decision has been queued throughout the pause.

What enters the TRICARE system in compressed sequence after the BAP reconstitutes is not one decision. It is a year of decisions.

## The legal architecture

The statutory frame matters because it explains why the storm cannot be slow-rolled. The Secretary of Defense, and through delegated authority the Director of the Defense Health Agency, does not have administrative discretion to release formulary decisions absent BAP comment. Implementing authority is conditioned on the BAP having performed its statutory function.

Under 10 U.S.C. § 1074g(c), the Secretary established the BAP to review and comment on the development of the uniform formulary. The statute directs that the Secretary "shall consider the comments of the panel before implementing the uniform formulary or implementing changes to the uniform formulary." The implementing regulation at 32 CFR 199.21 builds out the procedural mechanics for the TRICARE Pharmacy Benefits Program, including the UF process and BAP comment review at §199.21(e)(3). Both authorities run together. Both apply.

The BAP charter, last renewed August 1, 2024 at 89 FR 62726, fixes the Panel at no more than fifteen members appointed by the Secretary of Defense. The charter expires August 1, 2026. The Federal Advisory Committee Act governs the meetings under 5 U.S.C. chapter 10. The current Designated Federal Officer of record is CAPT Tiffany F. Cline, USN. Dr. Pamela Schweitzer served as Chair through the Panel's last meeting on December 18, 2024; her appointment, along with those of all other BAP members, concluded April 25, 2025. No successor has been publicly named.

What the freeze created is the legal equivalent of a queue with no release valve. P&T continued meeting. Recommendations sat in queue, not as proposed changes, but as completed P&T actions awaiting the procedural step required by federal law before they become operative.

The May 6-7 P&T meeting deepens that queue.

---

## The pressure release

The mechanics of release are not symmetrical. Even if the BAP is reconstituted before the August 1, 2026 charter expiration, the Panel cannot retroactively review every queued P&T recommendation in a single meeting. A standard BAP agenda runs roughly three hours and covers a limited number of therapeutic class reviews and newly approved drug categories per session.

Two procedural paths exist for the backlog. The Panel can hold expanded sessions, take agenda items in compressed sequence, or schedule additional meetings outside the standard quarterly cadence to work through the queue. Or the Department can take a position that some queued recommendations are stale and require fresh P&T review before BAP can comment on them, restarting the clock for those classes.

Both paths produce a different version of the storm. The expanded-session path drops a year of decisions on the federal pharmacy benefit at high velocity. The fresh-P&T path delays meaningful release further, extending the freeze in functional terms while complying with procedural form.

The first signal will come from how DoD reconstitutes BAP membership and what notice, if any, appears in the Federal Register for a 2026 BAP meeting. Charter expiration on August 1, 2026 sets a procedural cliff. Either the charter is renewed and members are appointed in time to convene, or the freeze deepens into a charter lapse.

## The layered events

The storm does not arrive on an empty calendar.

January 1, 2026 brought the largest TRICARE pharmacy copayment increase in years. For Home Delivery 90-day supply, brand-name copays rose from $38 to $44. Non-formulary copays rose from $76 to $85. Retail network 30-day brand-name went from $43 to $48. The increase took effect months before any pathway opens to clear the queue. Beneficiaries are already paying the new copays against the old formulary. The new formulary, when it lands, will determine which prescriptions move to which copay tier with the new rates already in effect.

The proposed Combat and Operational Medicine Program and Private Sector Care Program split for FY2027 changes how Defense Health Program funds flow to pharmacy operations. The President's Budget request, not yet enacted, proposes ending the legacy DHP appropriation and replacing it with two new appropriations: COMP at $20.341B discretionary plus $3.139B mandatory facilities sustainment, and PSCP at $22.175B discretionary. April's DHA reorganization created acting-status leadership in offices most directly downstream of formulary policy. The Acting Assistant Director for Health Care Administration is RDML Matthew Case. The Acting Assistant Director for Support is Kathleen Berst. Integration boards that survived the reorganization are still building cadence. PEO DHMS executive management authority over the IT infrastructure that runs the federal pharmacy benefit retains its scope, but workflows around formulary file updates, contract administration, and beneficiary communication run through other offices still finding their post-reorganization rhythm.

The Express Scripts FTC settlement of February 4, 2026 reshaped the pharmacy benefit manager's compensation structure, eliminating list-price-tied incentives, capping insulin at $25 per month for eligible members under qualifying plans, and integrating TrumpRx into Express Scripts' standard offering contingent on relevant legal and regulatory changes. TRICARE's pharmacy benefit runs through Express Scripts. Settlement implementation is concurrent with whatever formulary backlog release path the Department selects. Beneficiary-facing communication about which drugs are subject to which set of pricing rules will need to differentiate FTC-mandated changes from formulary-mandated changes for a population that already finds the pharmacy benefit difficult to navigate.

The formulary storm is one variable in a system absorbing four simultaneous changes at the operational layer.

## Who feels it

The downstream populations worth naming.

**Active-duty service members and their dependents.** Tier shifts and step therapy changes affect prescribing patterns across the Direct Care system. MTF clinicians prescribing into the federal formulary will need real-time updates as the queue releases. Time between BAP comment, Secretary decision, and operational implementation in the TRICARE Online formulary file is the window where prescribing recommendations may not match what the system will actually fill. Service members in remote operational settings or on TRICARE Prime referrals face the steepest navigation cost.

**Retirees and their families.** TRICARE for Life beneficiaries and TRICARE Select retirees rely on stable formulary signals to manage chronic disease. The retiree population is older, more medication-complex, and more sensitive to copay differentials than the active-duty population. A queue release that moves a chronic disease drug from Tier 1 to Tier 2 changes the household pharmacy budget at the exact moment the new copay rates are already in effect.

**Specialty disease populations.** Oncology, rheumatology, autoimmune, and rare disease patients on TRICARE depend on prior authorization criteria that update through P&T cycles. A year of frozen criteria means a year of prior authorizations adjudicated against the prior framework. As the new criteria release, providers and patients face renewal cycles where the rules have changed mid-treatment. The clinical risk lives in the renewal documentation that may not match what was approved twelve months earlier.

**Pharmaceutical manufacturers and government affairs teams.** New product launches that hit the FDA approval window during the freeze have been waiting for first federal formulary placement. The release sequence determines which products land first, which manufacturers receive the first beneficiary prescriptions through TRICARE, and which competitive positioning establishes early market share inside the federal pharmacy benefit. Manufacturers without a clear read on the release schedule cannot set sales force priorities, contracting timelines, or supply-chain commitments to TRICARE.

**Federal health contractors and PBM-adjacent vendors.** Express Scripts is the prime, but the surrounding ecosystem of utilization management, specialty pharmacy distribution, and clinical decision support contractors all run on formulary data feeds that have to update in compressed sequence. Software vendors providing clinical pharmacy support to MTFs need to load new criteria into clinical decision support tools as BAP-approved decisions release. The integration burden is concurrent with the DHA reorganization's IT integration board work.

The retiree in Tampa is the human face. The system the retiree interacts with has fifteen vendor seams running underneath that face, and the year of queued formulary decisions will hit each seam in sequence once the procedural pathway reopens.

## The 90-day watch

What to track from May 6 forward.

**The May 6-7 P&T agenda and recommendations** publish at health.mil under the P&T Committee meeting reference center. P&T does not publish full minutes the way BAP does, but the meeting reference center posts background information and committee recommendations for each cycle. Track for therapeutic class reviews on the agenda, newly approved drugs reviewed for first placement, and signals of how the Department is sequencing work that will eventually face BAP.

**Federal Register notices for BAP charter renewal and member appointments.** The current charter expires August 1, 2026. Notice of renewal would publish in the Federal Register. Member appointments would follow. Cadence and content of these notices is the leading indicator of when, or whether, BAP convenes again.

**Federal Register notices for BAP meetings.** A FACA-required notice precedes any open BAP meeting by approximately fifteen days. If a notice publishes in May, June, or July, the backlog clearance is starting. If no notice publishes through August, the freeze has functionally extended into a charter lapse.

**TRICARE pharmacy bulletin updates** at health.mil and on the Express Scripts member portal. These are operational signals that a BAP-approved decision has cleared the Secretary and is implementing into the formulary file. Cadence over the next ninety days reveals the actual release rate once the pathway reopens.

**Beneficiary communication frequency.** MOAA, FRA, NAUS, NMFA, and other beneficiary-representing organizations publish member communications when formulary changes affect their populations. Volume and specificity track operational impact.

**MTF formulary committee minutes** at the local level. Service-level pharmacy committees and MTF P&T equivalents respond to federal formulary changes by adjusting local protocols. Local committee activity is the leading indicator of how the federal release is hitting clinical operations.

## The handoff

The retiree in Tampa picks up his prescription. The price he pays is governed by a copay tier set by a formulary file that may release a different tier within ninety days of the procedural pathway reopening. His prescriber operates under a prior authorization criterion that may release a different criterion within the same window. His pharmacist runs the transaction against an Express Scripts ruleset shaped by the FTC settlement that took effect in February.

None of those changes are wrong on their own terms. Advisory committee review responded to a real executive priority. Copay increases responded to real budget pressure. The FTC settlement responded to a real antitrust concern. DHA reorganization responded to a real statutory obligation. Each one, taken alone, has a defensible rationale.

Four changes hitting the same population in the same quarter, while a year of formulary decisions queues for release into the same pharmacy benefit, is the storm.

What put the storm here now is that a procedural pause swept up a statutorily-required advisory body the pause architecture did not appear to anticipate. What keeps it largely invisible outside DHA pharmacy operations is that the federal pharmacy benefit absorbs operational disruption quietly. Beneficiaries notice when their copay changes. They rarely connect that change to the architecture that produced it.

The May 6-7 P&T meeting deepens the queue. The August 1 charter expiration sets a procedural cliff. The next ninety days reveal whether DoD reconstitutes BAP in time to begin clearing the backlog or extends the freeze into a structural condition. Senior federal health audiences should treat the period from May 6 through the end of August 2026 as the operational window where federal pharmacy benefit policy is being made, and in some cases not made, in compressed sequence with reduced visibility.

The retiree pays his copay and walks out. Behind the counter the system is paused. He has no signal that it is.

Let's roll.
