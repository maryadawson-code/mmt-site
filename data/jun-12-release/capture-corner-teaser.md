# Capture Corner is live: The Expert Test

Founding Members,

An AI-built pricing sheet priced a master's-degree professional with ten years at 47 percent below the GSA rate, and a hundred AI-generated questions landed on a subcontractor for whom not one of them fit. The public issue, "The Expert You Cannot Buy Off the Shelf," makes the argument: the model is now the cheap part, and the expert whose judgment makes it safe is the product, not the cost the agent removes.

The Capture Corner turns that argument into the checks you run this week, and it is up on the site now. Here is what is in the full brief:

- **The three-rung diagnostic.** Chat seat, project, or agent: the table that names what you actually have, the tell for each rung, and what each can be trusted with. Most "AI adoption" is a chat seat, and a chat seat carries none of the judgment a bid needs.
- **The SME-inside test.** Six questions, with required evidence, that find out whose judgment is inside any proposal or capture agent. A vendor who answers in model names and benchmarks is selling you a chat window.
- **The pricing pressure-test.** The 47-percent failure turned into a procedure: walk every AI-built rate against the current GSA schedule and the staffing math, plus the four red flags that should stop a sheet cold before FAR 15.404-1 cost realism stops it for you.
- **The human-gate standard.** Who reads what before AI content ships, the relevance check that would have caught the hundred questions, and the override-rate metric that proves the gate is real and not a rubber stamp.
- **The nine agent-security questions.** The lethal trifecta, scoped identity, runtime gating, hijacking posture, shadow agents, the FAR gap, and model plurality, in writing, for any vendor selling you an "agent."
- **Build, buy, and the small-business trap.** When to encode your own SME's judgment versus buy commodity workflow, and the one review a three-person shop can least afford to skip.
